\title{Topological Hallucination Detectors on Decoder Attention Reduce to First-Order Statistics}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Persistent homology (PH) of attention graphs underlies a family of hallucination detectors, including TOHA \citep{toha}. We show that in decoder-only models these topological features reduce to \emph{first-order} attention statistics --- quantities read off single rows or columns of the attention matrix, such as a row maximum or one token's attention to another --- either exactly or up to one computable number. For any symmetric dissimilarity and vertex $s$ we define the \emph{coning defect} $\defect_s$; classical cone and stability arguments then place the whole Vietoris--Rips barcode within bottleneck distance $\defect_s$ of a ``star diagram'' read off the distances to $s$, so every stable vectorization is a function of one column of the attention matrix, up to $\defect_s$. The same argument applies to TOHA: its prompt--response topological divergence equals one minus the response tokens' mean largest prompt attention whenever a prompt-level coning defect vanishes, and lies within that defect otherwise. Across \NSettings{} settings (\NModels{} open models of \ModelSizeRange{}; TruthfulQA, HaluEval, and on-policy TriviaQA) the two-sided bound holds without violation on \TohaCells{} per-head graphs and collapses to an exact equality on \TohaConedRange{} of them, including in two models with no first-token sink, where the statistic is the largest prompt attention rather than sink attention. Adding TOHA's score to that first-order counterpart changes AUC by at most \TohaLFMaxpMax{}, and supervised probes on the two are equivalent in every setting. On \NCellsTotal{} layer graphs there are no bound violations; in every model with a first-token sink, \PctConedRange{} of graphs are exactly coned with empty $H_1$, and length-normalized $0$D persistence keeps a median per-layer rank correlation of at least \RhoMin{} with sink attention. For detection, $0$D PH, sink-deflated PH and the per-head coning defect add at most \TopoNTMaxAbs{} AUC over cheap non-topological probes, and TOHA at most \TohaNTMaxAll{}. We release the extractor, the evaluator, and per-layer features for a subset of settings.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A growing class of hallucination detectors views a transformer's attention matrix as a weighted graph, and then summarize it using persistent homology (PH), especially the barcodes of its connected components ($H_0$) and loops ($H_1$) along a Vietoris--Rips filtration \citep{kushnareva2021,cherniavskii2022,perez2022topological,kostenok2023,toha,samaga2026halluzig}. Their intuition is that perhaps global features in the flow of information could be detected by topology, which cannot be captured by statistics on tokens alone. In a parallel development, it has been shown that decoder-only language models devote a high proportion of their attention to the first token, known as the attention sink \citep{xiao2023efficient,sun2024massive,cancedda2024spectral,gu2025attentionsink,barbero2025first}. \citet{binkowski2026sinkprobe} recently showed that sinks carry much of the signal attention-based detectors use: exactly for the spectral LapEigvals \citep{binkowski2025lapeigvals}, whose Laplacian entries are sink scores minus self-attention, and qualitatively for TOHA, whose minimum spanning tree sink nodes are ``likely'' to enter. Which part of a barcode the sink fixes, and by how much the rest can deviate, was open; Theorem~\ref{thm:cone} and Proposition~\ref{prop:toha} answer this exactly.

We show that, for topological detectors built from the Vietoris--Rips persistence or minimum spanning tree of a single attention graph --- the standard construction, and TOHA's --- the two literatures largely study the same object; zigzag persistence across layers and learned graph models are out of scope (\S\ref{sec:discussion}). When one vertex $s$ is closer to every other vertex than those vertices are to each other, every Vietoris--Rips sublevel set is a cone with apex $s$: higher homology vanishes and the minimum spanning tree (which computes $H_0$) is the star around $s$. A sink puts real attention graphs there, or close to it, so in most layers their ``topology'' is a function of each token's attention to the sink, and elsewhere it deviates by at most one computable number, the coning defect. How close is an empirical matter, measured here at short-form QA lengths; long retrieved contexts, where the defect has more room to grow, are untested (\S\ref{sec:discussion}). The same holds for TOHA, whose divergence is a first-order statistic plus a computable correction.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{A whole-barcode sink reduction} (\S\ref{sec:theory}). The ingredients are classical: a flag complex with a dominating vertex is a cone \citep{barmak2012strong,boissonnat2020edge}, and persistence is stable \citep{cohensteiner2007stability,chazal2009proximity,chazal2014geometric}. We combine them into one-sided bounds in a single computable number. For any symmetric dissimilarity and vertex $s$, bars of dimension $k\ge1$ have length at most $\defect_s$, and $0$D total persistence is sandwiched around the star weight (Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}). The whole barcode then lies within bottleneck distance $\defect_s$ of the star diagram ($\defect_s/2$ of a shifted one), so all stable vectorizations are equivalent to a single column of distances (Corollary~\ref{cor:diagram}). For causal attention, $\defect_0$ has a closed form (Corollary~\ref{cor:causal}).
\item \textbf{The published topological detector is a first-order detector} (\S\ref{sec:theory}, \S\ref{sec:toha}). Proposition~\ref{prop:toha}: The topological divergence of TOHA is one minus the average max-prompt attention of the response tokens on prompt-coned heads or is within a computable defect of it. The qualitative observation by \citet{binkowski2026sinkprobe} is made exact. There are no violations of the identity on \TohaCells{} real head graphs, and adding TOHA to its first-order counterpart changes AUC by \TohaLFMaxpRange{}.
\item \textbf{Verification of the whole-graph reduction} (\S\ref{sec:reduction}, Appendix~\ref{sec:onedim}). There are no bound violations in \NCellsTotal{} graphs, and graphs are exactly coned in \PctConedRange{} of cases in sink-dominated models. In a sink dose-response study, we explain how $1$D features become weak because of coning, while $H_1$ is empty on all coned graphs.
\item \textbf{A detection study using strong baselines} (\S\ref{sec:detection}). We compare $0$D/$1$D PH, sink features, sink-deflated PH, the coning defect, and TOHA against length, lexical, log-probability, LLM-Check \citep{sriramanan2024llmcheck}, Lookback-Lens \citep{chuang2024lookback}, and hidden-state probes. We report results from TruthfulQA, HaluEval, and an on-policy TriviaQA benchmark using grouped cross-validation, early- and late-fusion incremental tests, and equivalence tests.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Topology of attention.} \citet{kushnareva2021} and \citet{cherniavskii2022} introduced barcodes of symmetrized BERT attention graphs as features for artificial-text detection and acceptability; \citet{perez2022topological} and \citet{kostenok2023} extended them to classification and uncertainty estimation. TOHA \citep{toha} calculates an MST-based distance between prompt and response subgraphs for hallucination detection, HalluZig \citep{samaga2026halluzig} relies on zigzag persistence across layers, and CHARM \citep{charm} learns a message-passing model over attention graphs. \citet{gardinazzi2024persistent} study zigzag persistence of hidden representations. All $0$D summaries in this family are MST statistics (Fact~\ref{fact:mst}; \citealp{gower1969}). Our results concern what those statistics measure in causal models.

\paragraph{Attention sinks.} \citet{xiao2023efficient} observed that streaming LMs keep the first tokens' key-values because a large share of attention lands there; \citet{sun2024massive} tied sinks to massive activations, \citet{cancedda2024spectral} to low-rank ``dark signals'', and \citet{gu2025attentionsink} characterized when they emerge during pre-training. \citet{barbero2025first} argued they prevent over-mixing; related ``no-op'' attention to special tokens was observed in BERT \citep{clark2019bert} and vision transformers \citep{darcet2024registers}. \citet{binkowski2026sinkprobe} use sinks with large-norm values directly as a hallucination signal (SinkProbe) and show that earlier detectors rely on them, exactly for LapEigvals (whose Laplacian eigenvalues, for causal attention, are its diagonal entries) and qualitatively for LLM-Check, Lookback-Lens and TOHA. For topological detectors we make this exact, and for TOHA the reduced statistic is max-prompt attention, which is sink attention only in sink-dominated heads.

\paragraph{Hallucination detection.} Beyond topology, detectors use sampling consistency \citep{manakul2023selfcheckgpt,farquhar2024semantic}, internal probes \citep{azaria2023internal,burns2022discovering,marks2023geometry,li2023iti,kossen2024semantic,orgad2024llms,du2024haloscope}, covariance spectra \citep{chen2024inside}, attention-kernel log-determinants \citep{sriramanan2024llmcheck}, and context-vs-generated attention ratios \citep{chuang2024lookback}. Recent audits find that several benchmarks admit near-perfect detection from surface form alone \citep{hussain2026parallax,janiak2025illusion}, echoing annotation artifacts in NLI \citep{gururangan2018annotation,mccoy2019right}. We include lexical controls and an on-policy benchmark.

\section{Theory: attention sinks cone the Vietoris--Rips filtration}
\label{sec:theory}

Let $D\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^{N\times N}$ be symmetric with zero diagonal. The Vietoris--Rips filtration is $K_\epsilon=\{\sigma\subseteq[N]:\max_{u,v\in\sigma}D_{uv}\le\epsilon\}$; $\dgm_k(D)$ is the barcode of $H_k(K_\epsilon;\mathbb{F})$ over a field, and $P_k(D)$ is its total persistence (sum of finite bar lengths). For attention, $A^{(l)}$ is the head-averaged, row-stochastic, lower-triangular attention matrix of layer $l$ and, following the literature, $D=1-\max(A,A^\top)$ with zero diagonal.

\begin{fact}[\citealp{gower1969}]
\label{fact:mst}
All $0$D bars are born at $0$, and their finite death times are, as a multiset, the edge weights of a minimum spanning tree (MST) of $D$. In particular $P_0(D)=w(\mathrm{MST}(D))$ is the total persistence (sum of finite bar lengths) of $\dgm_0(D)$.
\end{fact}

\begin{definition}[Coning defect, star weight]
For $s\in[N]$, the \emph{coning defect} is $\defect_s(D)=\max_{u\ne v,\;u,v\ne s}\big[\max(D_{su},D_{sv})-D_{uv}\big]_+$ (zero if $N<3$), and the \emph{star weight} is $\stw_s(D)=\sum_{u\ne s}D_{su}$. We call $D$ \emph{coned at $s$} if $\defect_s(D)=0$, i.e.\ $s$ is at least as close to both endpoints of every edge as they are to each other.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Sink-coning reduction]
\label{thm:cone}
For every symmetric $D$ and every vertex $s$, with $\defect=\defect_s(D)$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
\item every bar of $\dgm_k(D)$, $k\ge1$, has length at most $\defect$; hence $P_k(D)\le\defect\cdot|\dgm_k(D)|$;
\item $\stw_s(D)-(N-2)\,\defect\;\le\;P_0(D)\;\le\;\stw_s(D)$, and $\max_u D_{su}-\defect\le\max \dgm_0^{\text{fin}}(D)\le\max_u D_{su}$;
\item if $D$ is coned at $s$, then $H_k(K_\epsilon)=0$ for all $k\ge1$ and all $\epsilon$, the star at $s$ is an MST, and $P_0(D)=\stw_s(D)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch (full proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs})]
Let $D'_{uv}=\max(D_{uv},D_{su},D_{sv})$ for $u,v\ne s$ and $D'_{su}=D_{su}$. Then $D\le D'\le D+\defect$ entrywise and $D'$ is coned at $s$. Since $D'$ is coned, any simplex $\sigma$ with $s\notin\sigma$ and $|\sigma|\ge2$ has the same filtration value as $\sigma\cup\{s\}$, and a vertex not yet joined to $s$ has no edges; so each $K'_\epsilon$ is a cone with apex $s$ plus isolated points, and $H_k(K'_\epsilon)=0$ for $k\ge1$. The inclusions $K_b\subseteq K'_{b+\defect}\subseteq K_{b+\defect}$ factor the persistence map $H_k(K_b)\to H_k(K_{b+\defect})$ through zero, so no bar outlives $\defect$. For (b), the star is a spanning tree of $D$ (upper bound) and an MST of $D'$. Every spanning tree has an edge at $s$, where $D'=D$, so passing from $D'$ to $D$ lowers a tree's weight by at most $(N{-}2)\defect$; the bottleneck value is $1$-Lipschitz in the sup-norm. The ingredients are standard: Lemma~\ref{lem:coned} is the dominating-vertex (cone) argument behind strong and edge collapses \citep{barmak2012strong,boissonnat2020edge}, and the last step is a one-sided form of the interleaving argument behind persistence stability \citep{chazal2009proximity}.
\end{proof}

The bound in (a) is attained: if $D_{su}=1$ for all $u$ and four further vertices form a $4$-cycle with side $\tfrac12$ and diagonals $1$, then $\defect_s=\tfrac12$ and $H_1$ has the single bar $[\tfrac12,1)$. On real attention the bounds are not vacuous (Table~\ref{tab:boundgap}): the longest $H_1$ bar reaches $\GapUOneMax{}\,\defect_0$, and (b) is uninformative on at most \GapVacMax{} of non-coned graphs.

Theorem~\ref{thm:cone} controls summaries. The same comparison controls the whole barcode. Call $\Sigma_s(D)$ the \emph{star diagram}: in degree $0$ the bars $[0,D_{su})$, $u\ne s$, plus one infinite bar, and nothing in degree $k\ge1$.

\begin{corollary}[The whole barcode is the star, up to $\defect_s$]
\label{cor:diagram}
In every degree $k\ge1$, $d_B\big(\dgm_k(D),\emptyset\big)\le\defect_s/2$. In degree $0$, the $j$-th smallest finite death of $D$ lies in $[D_{s(j)}-\defect_s,\,D_{s(j)}]$, where $D_{s(j)}$ is the $j$-th smallest entry of $(D_{su})_{u\ne s}$, and $\beta_0$ of $K_\epsilon$ lies between $1+\#\{u:D_{su}>\epsilon+\defect_s\}$ and $1+\#\{u:D_{su}>\epsilon\}$. Hence $d_B\big(\dgm(D),\Sigma_s(D)\big)\le\defect_s$, and $d_B\big(\dgm(D),\Sigma_s^{\defect}(D)\big)\le\defect_s/2$ for the shifted star diagram $\Sigma_s^{\defect}$ with finite $0$D deaths $(D_{s(j)}-\defect_s/2)_+$; both diagrams are equal to $\dgm(D)$ when $\defect_s=0$. Every vectorization that is $L$-Lipschitz in $d_B$ (e.g.\ persistence landscapes; \citealp{bubenik2015landscapes}) is therefore within $L\defect_s/2$ of a function of the column $(D_{su})_u$ and the number $\defect_s$, and within $L\defect_s$ of a function of the column alone. Vectorizations that are stable in $1$-Wasserstein distance (e.g.\ persistence images; \citealp{adams2017persistence}) are within $L\big((N{-}2)\defect_s+\tfrac12\defect_s\sum_{k\ge1}|\dgm_k(D)|\big)$ of a function of the column. On coned graphs, every vectorization (such as persistence entropy or Betti curves) is precisely such a function.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Causal attention]
\label{cor:causal}
Let $A$ be lower-triangular and row-stochastic, $D=1-\max(A,A^\top)$, and $s=0$ the first token. Then $D_{0u}=1-A_{u0}$, so $\stw_0=(N-1)(1-\bar m)$ with $\bar m=\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{u\ge1}A_{u0}$ the mean sink attention, and
\[
\defect_0=\max_{1\le v<u}\big[A_{uv}-\min(A_{u0},A_{v0})\big]_+ .
\]
Thus $P_0\in[(N-1)(1-\bar m)-(N-2)\defect_0,\,(N-1)(1-\bar m)]$ and all $H_{k\ge1}$ bars have length at most $\defect_0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Choice of distance]
The graph is exactly coned iff, for all $1\le v<u$, $A_{uv}\le A_{u0}$ and $A_{uv}\le A_{v0}$: no token attends to an earlier non-sink token more than either of the two attends to the sink.
Coning is an order condition, so it is invariant under any entrywise strictly increasing transform $f$ of $D$ with $f(0)=0$ (e.g.\ $-\log$ of the symmetrized attention, or ranks without ties, in place of $1-A$). The set of exactly coned graphs, and the vanishing of their higher homology, does not depend on how attention is turned into a distance. Only the magnitude of $\defect_s$ on non-coned graphs does. (A merely non-decreasing $f$, such as clipping, can create coning.)
\end{remark}

Corollary~\ref{cor:causal} makes four testable predictions: \textbf{(C1)} in coned layers $0$D total persistence equals $(N{-}1)(1-\bar m)$, so after dividing by $N-1$ it is an affine function of sink attention; \textbf{(C2)} $H_1$ is empty whenever $\defect_0=0$; \textbf{(C3)} $1$D persistence can only be non-zero where the sink is violated, with bars no longer than $\defect_0$; \textbf{(C4)} removing the sink and renormalizing (deflation) exposes whatever non-sink topology exists.

\paragraph{Random weights and directed complexes.} For i.i.d.\ weights, on the other hand, $1$D total persistence is known to scale linearly with $N$ \citep{hiraoka2017msa,hino2019lifetime}, and we give an elementary explicit bound (Proposition~\ref{prop:morse}, Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}): $\mathbb{E}[P_1]\ge\tfrac{3N}{8}-\sqrt{3N}+O(1)$. We prove that the directed flag complex of the digraph is not a way out for causal attention: the digraph is a transitive tournament, so its weighted directed flag complex is isomorphic to $\VR(D)$ as a filtered complex (Proposition~\ref{thm:directed}, Appendix~\ref{app:directed}).

\paragraph{The published topological detector reduces to a first-order statistic.} TOHA \citep{toha} scores an attention head by the topological divergence between the response tokens $R$ and the prompt tokens $P$. Its per-head score is $d(R,P)=\mathrm{MTopDiv}(R,P)/|R|$, where $\mathrm{MTopDiv}$ is the total $0$D persistence of the head's graph $D=1-\max(A,A^\top)$ after all prompt--prompt distances are set to $0$ (equivalently, the minimum spanning forest attaching $R$ to $P$). TOHA averages $d$ over at most ten heads chosen on labeled data. For $u\in R$ let $\pi_u=\max_{v\in P}A_{uv}$ be $u$'s largest attention to any prompt token.

\begin{proposition}[TOHA is max-prompt attention, up to a defect]
\label{prop:toha}
Let $A$ be lower-triangular and row-stochastic, and let $P=\{0,\dots,p-1\}$ and $R=\{p,\dots,N-1\}$. With $\defect_P=\max_{w<u\in R}\big[A_{uw}-\min(\pi_u,\pi_w)\big]_+$,
\[
\frac1{|R|}\sum_{u\in R}(1-\pi_u)\;-\;\frac{|R|-1}{|R|}\,\defect_P\;\le\; d(R,P)\;\le\;\frac1{|R|}\sum_{u\in R}(1-\pi_u),
\]
with equality when $\defect_P=0$. Moreover $1-\pi_u\le 1-A_{u0}$, with equality iff the first token is among $u$'s most-attended prompt tokens. On a head where this holds for every $u\in R$ and $\defect_P=0$, $d(R,P)=1-\frac1{|R|}\sum_{u\in R}A_{u0}$, the response tokens' mean sink attention.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Zeroing the prompt block merges $P$ at scale $0$, so the remaining finite $0$D bars are the MST weights of the quotient graph on $\{p^*\}\cup R$, where $D_{p^*u}=\min_{v\in P}D_{uv}=1-\pi_u$ (Fact~\ref{fact:mst}). Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}(b) at the apex $p^*$; as in Corollary~\ref{cor:causal}, its coning defect is $\defect_P$ and its star weight is $\sum_u(1-\pi_u)$. Divide by $|R|$.
\end{proof}
\noindent \citet{binkowski2026sinkprobe} note that sink nodes are ``likely to appear in the MST'' of $\mathrm{MTopDiv}$. Proposition~\ref{prop:toha} makes this exact, and it indicates where the topology can enter: only through $\defect_P$, which is non-zero only when a response token attends to an earlier response token more than one of the two attends to its best prompt token. We test the proposition on real heads, and TOHA against its first-order counterparts, in \S\ref{sec:toha}.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Models and benchmarks.} We use Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B/7B-Instruct, Phi-3-mini-4k-instruct, TinyLlama-1.1B-Chat, SmolLM-1.7B-Instruct, and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2: \NModels{} open models spanning \ModelSizeRange{} parameters, with additional Mistral-7B analysis in Appendix~\ref{app:mistral}. Mistral is run twice, with its hand-written \texttt{[INST]} string and with its own chat template; as a consistency check the two differ by one response token and agree to \MistralFmtOtherGap{} AUC on every bank except answer-only PH (\MistralFmtAnsGap{}; Appendix~\ref{app:impl}). \textbf{TruthfulQA} \citep{lin2022truthfulqa}: all 817 questions, each paired with its best answer and first incorrect answer ($1{,}634$ rows) under each model's chat format (Appendix~\ref{app:impl}). \textbf{HaluEval QA} \citep{li2024halueval}: the first $1{,}000$ (Qwen2.5-3B) or $500$ (Qwen2.5-1.5B) knowledge-grounded questions with right and hallucinated answers. \textbf{TriviaQA on-policy}: $2{,}000$ random validation questions \citep{joshi2017triviaqa}; each model answers greedily (short-phrase instruction, $\le24$ tokens); answer labeled hallucinated iff it does not include a normalized gold alias; features are extracted from the model's own answer (accuracies in Table~\ref{tab:onpolicy}). All models run in bfloat16 with eager attention.

\paragraph{Features.} Per layer (head-averaged $A$): $0$D (max lifetime, total persistence); $1$D (max lifetime, total persistence, and the birth and the death of the longest bar, each divided by $N$); \textsc{Sink} ($\stw_0$ and $\max_u D_{0u}$, i.e.\ exactly the two quantities Corollary~\ref{cor:causal} says $0$D reduces to); \textsc{Deflated} PH ($0$D+$1$D after deleting token 0 and renormalizing rows); \textsc{Answer-only} PH (induced subgraph on answer tokens); the coning defect ($\defect_0$ and $\min_s\defect_s$); row entropy and row max. Per head: $0$D (MST total and max via a dense Prim's algorithm), sink mass, the coning defect $\defect_0$ of the head's graph (\textsc{Defect}$_h$), entropy, Lookback ratio \citep{chuang2024lookback}, and the LLM-Check attention score \citep{sriramanan2024llmcheck}. \textsc{TOHA} (per head, separate forward pass, same rows): the exact score $d$ of \citet{toha} via a dense Prim's algorithm on the prompt-contracted graph, the $\bar\pi$-score, the response sink score, and $\defect_P$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:toha}). Other: answer-token log-probability and predictive-entropy statistics; mean-pooled answer hidden states at the middle layer; TF-IDF word and character $n$-grams of the answer text (\textsc{Lexical}). Only the $1$D summaries are length-normalized: $0$D and \textsc{Sink} enter the classifier raw, so the two banks the reduction pairs carry the same length information, with \textsc{Len} scored separately as a control.

\paragraph{Protocol.} All numbers use one evaluator: standardized features and $L_2$ logistic regression, with $C=1$ for banks of at most 64 dimensions and otherwise $C\in\{0.01,0.1,1\}$ chosen by inner grouped 3-fold CV; widening that grid to $\{10^{-4},\dots,10^{2}\}$ helps the topological banks more (up to \CGridTopoMax{}) than the non-topological ones (\CGridNTMax{}) and reverses none of the orderings we claim (Appendix~\ref{app:impl}). The outer loop is 10-fold \texttt{StratifiedGroupKFold} grouped by question, repeated over 3 seeds (42--44), with out-of-fold probabilities averaged. We test incremental value in two ways. Early fusion (``X$\mid$Y'') concatenates the banks. Late fusion (``X$\oplus$Y'') stacks the two banks' out-of-fold logits in a second-level logistic regression under the same CV, so that a small bank cannot be drowned out by a wide one. Differences in pooled AUC use a $10{,}000$-resample question-level cluster bootstrap. We call two banks equivalent if the 90\% interval lies inside $\pm0.015$ AUC (two one-sided tests, TOST, at $\alpha=0.05$), and report TOST power. Paired benchmarks also report within-pair accuracy. The theory checks use all (example, layer) graphs. Ripser \citep{bauer2021ripser} represents its filtration values as 32-bit floats, so we check the bar-length and max-death bounds at tolerance $10^{-5}$, and the $P_0$ sandwich, a sum of up to $N$ such values, at $10^{-3}$.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\RESULTSDISCUSSION

\paragraph{Limitations.} Our scales go \ModelSizeRange{}; sink strength varies with scale and training \citep{gu2025attentionsink}, so the coned fraction is model-specific, though Theorem~\ref{thm:cone} is not. HalluZig's zigzag and learned graph models \citep{samaga2026halluzig,charm} are out of scope, as are sampling-based (semantic entropy; \citealp{farquhar2024semantic}) and latent-probe (CCS; \citealp{burns2022discovering}) baselines, which score quantities other than the attention graph. We do not reproduce TOHA on its RAG benchmarks (\S\ref{sec:toha}): whether the reduction stays exact on long retrieved contexts, where $\defect_P$ may grow, is untested. On-policy labels use alias matching; an LLM-judge audit finds \AuditDisagreeRange{} disagreement without changing the orderings we claim (Appendix~\ref{app:validation}).
