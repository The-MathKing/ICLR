\begin{abstract}
Persistent homology on transformer attention graphs has recently emerged as a geometric approach for detecting hallucinations in Large Language Models (LLMs). We show that its most widely used component is a classical graph quantity in disguise: $0$-dimensional ($H_0$) Vietoris--Rips persistence on symmetrized attention distance graphs is identical as a multiset to Kruskal's Minimum Spanning Tree (MST) under single-linkage clustering (\textbf{Theorem 1}), a classical mathematical equivalence \citep{gower1969, carlsson2009} whose practical consequence for attention-graph interpretability has gone unexploited. We package this into \textbf{MST-TDA}, a lightweight feature extractor that exactly reproduces $0$D persistence without any persistent-homology library dependency and, when extended to per-head resolution, captures predictive signal that head-averaging discards ($0.892$ vs.\ $0.677$ AUC on a held-out HaluEval subset; an accuracy gain arising from per-head resolution, not from MST differing numerically from $0$D persistence, which are identical by construction). Crucially, against $0$D Ripser alone, MST-TDA offers no speedup, since $0$D Ripser is already a union-find over sorted edges (i.e.\ Kruskal's algorithm); the three-orders-of-magnitude computational savings ($>\!1{,}200\times$ at $N{=}256$ tokens) arise strictly from omitting the expensive $1$D homology reduction step.

We then audit whether the field's other headline component, $1$-dimensional cyclic persistence ($H_1$), earns its compute cost. We provide a mechanical argument (\textbf{Conjecture 1}) explaining why $1$D persistence scales with sequence length $N$ via simplicial clique expansion, together with empirical validation of super-linear scaling under both an i.i.d.\ null model and real attention graphs. Across seven benchmark settings spanning five open-weight model families at six model scales ($1.1$B--$7$B parameters: \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B}, \texttt{SmolLM-1.7B}, \texttt{Phi-3-mini-3.8B}, \texttt{Mistral-7B}, \texttt{TinyLlama-1.1B}) on \texttt{HaluEval QA} and \texttt{TruthfulQA} under strict grouped cross-validation, length-normalized $1$D persistence yields a negligible incremental gain on TruthfulQA (statistically indistinguishable from zero across model scales, with $99.99\%$ feature degeneracy on \texttt{Mistral-7B}, $\Delta\text{AUC}=-0.0002$) and a small apparent increment on HaluEval ($+0.010$ to $+0.016$ AUC). However, joint confound conditioning reveals that HaluEval answers exhibit an independent surface-form/lexical confound (AUC $0.96$--$0.98$ from answer text alone); when controlling jointly for length and lexical features, the $1$D increment on HaluEval collapses entirely ($\Delta\text{AUC} \le 0.000$). We note that cross-model comparisons are subject to benchmark-specific prompt template differences. A synthetic causal benchmark confirms full sensitivity to planted topology ($\text{ROC-AUC}=1.000$). We release MST-TDA, synthetic suites, and the full audit harness as reusable tools.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large Language Models (LLMs) frequently generate fluent yet factually incorrect statements, commonly termed hallucinations \citep{huang2023survey, ji2023survey}. Developing principled, unsupervised, or lightweight probes to identify hallucinations without expensive sampling rollouts remains a central challenge in trustworthy machine learning. Emerging methods span predictive entropy \citep{farquhar2024semantic}, internal hidden state linear probes \citep{azaria2023internal, chuang2024lookback}, and graph-based representations of internal transformer activations.

A parallel body of work has sought principled ways to operationalize LLM internal representations for interpretability. Functional decomposition methods such as Jet Expansions \citep{chen2026jets} show that a widely-used interpretability heuristic (Logit Lens) is a special case of a more general recursive decomposition of the residual stream -- demonstrating that a classical mathematical fact, once recognized, can subsume and generalize an entire line of empirical tooling. We take the same approach to attention-graph TDA below.

Recently, a growing lineage of literature has applied Topological Data Analysis (TDA) to transformer attention graphs. Seminal works demonstrated that 0-dimensional ($H_0$, connected components) and 1-dimensional ($H_1$, cyclic cavities) Vietoris--Rips persistent homology on attention maps yields predictive signal for text classification, grammar acceptability, and uncertainty estimation \citep{kushnareva2021, cherniavskii2022, kostenok2023}. More recently, methods such as TOHA \citep{toha} and CHARM \citep{charm} extended attention-graph geometric analysis specifically to hallucination detection in LLMs and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).

As illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:teaser}, what appears to be a sophisticated higher-order topological signal is, for its $0$-dimensional component, a classical graph quantity in disguise -- and for its $1$-dimensional component, a length confound in disguise. Recognizing this changes what the field should build and how it should evaluate: not a debunking, but a strictly cheaper, exact, and empirically stronger replacement.

Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Exact connection between $0$D attention TDA and Kruskal's MST, and a precise accounting of compute costs.} We recognize and formalize that $0$D persistence on symmetrized attention graphs is identically Kruskal's Minimum Spanning Tree under single-linkage clustering (Theorem~\ref{thm:mst}, connecting to \citet{gower1969, carlsson2009}). We provide a practical MST-based feature extractor (MST-TDA), verify its numerical identity to Ripser's $0$D output ($<10^{-12}$ float64 tolerance across $52{,}288$ comparisons), and extend it per-head ($0.892$ vs.\ $0.677$ AUC). We clarify that MST-TDA does not accelerate $0$D Ripser alone (both execute Kruskal's algorithm); rather, the $>\!1{,}200\times$ speedup at $N{=}256$ is achieved by eliminating the unnecessary $1$D homology reduction step (\S\ref{sec:method}).
    \item \textbf{Mechanical explanation and test of the $1$D length confound.} We provide an argument for why $1$D persistence scales with token sequence length $N$ via induced chordless cycles (Conjecture~\ref{prop:scaling}) and provide a leakage-free, per-fold normalization estimator $\hat\alpha$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:dag}).
    \item \textbf{Discovery and joint control of an independent surface-form confound in HaluEval QA.} Beyond sequence length, we show HaluEval's answer pairs are separable by answer character length and bag-of-words alone (AUC $0.96$--$0.98$). Jointly conditioning $1$D persistence on both length and lexical features demonstrates that the apparent $+0.015$ incremental AUC on HaluEval collapses entirely ($\Delta\text{AUC} \le 0.000$).
    \item \textbf{A synthetic causal benchmark (sensitivity verification).} To rule out pipeline insensitivity, we construct a controlled synthetic benchmark across six structural regimes (S1, S1b, S2--S5), achieving $\text{ROC-AUC}=1.000$ on planted topology (Positive Control) and on length-driven cycle growth (S1b), while exposing raw $1$D's collapse under unmediated length variation (S1).
    \item \textbf{A multi-model cost-benefit audit.} Across five model families at six scales ($1.1$B--$7$B parameters) on two benchmarks under grouped CV, length-normalized $1$D persistence yields no meaningful predictive gain on TruthfulQA and is absorbed by lexical artifacts on HaluEval. Against a $>\!1{,}200\times$ compute premium, $1$D homology fails a standard cost-benefit criterion.
\end{enumerate}
We release the MST extractor, synthetic benchmark suite, and full audit harness at \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/ICLR-BE07/}.

\section{Formal Setup, Identification Framework, and Hypotheses}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Mathematical Definitions}
Let $X = [x_1, \dots, x_N]$ be an input sequence of $N$ tokens processed by an autoregressive transformer. At layer $l \in \{1,\dots,L\}$, let $A^{(l)} \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times N}$ denote the head-averaged causal self-attention matrix, symmetrized as $W^{(l)} = \max(A^{(l)}, (A^{(l)})^T)$ into a distance matrix $D_{ij}^{(l)} = 1 - W_{ij}^{(l)}$ ($D_{ii}^{(l)}=0$). The Vietoris--Rips complex $\text{VR}(D^{(l)},\epsilon)$ at scale $\epsilon$ contains all subsets $\sigma$ with pairwise distance $\le \epsilon$; its $k$-dimensional persistence barcode $\text{dgm}_k(D^{(l)}) = \{(b_i,d_i)\}$ records the birth/death scales of $k$-dimensional cavities. Two feature representations $Z_1, Z_2$ are $(\epsilon,\mathcal{D})$-observationally equivalent under classifier family $\mathcal{F}$ and risk $\mathcal{R}$ if $|\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}} \mathcal{R}(f(Z_1)) - \inf_{g\in\mathcal{F}} \mathcal{R}(g(Z_2))| \le \epsilon$. Our audit rests on two load-bearing assumptions, standard in this literature: (i) attention is symmetrized before filtration ($W_{ij}=\max(A_{ij},A_{ji})$, discarding directionality -- see \S\ref{sec:discussion} for the directed alternative), and (ii) evaluation partitions are grouped so that $\text{Group}(x_i)\cap\text{Group}(x_j)=\emptyset$ for all $x_i\in X_{\text{train}}, x_j\in X_{\text{test}}$, preventing question-level leakage between paired grounded/hallucinated answers. Three further technical assumptions (teacher-forced extraction, monotonic distance inversion, single-linkage merge order) are stated in full in Appendix~\ref{app:assumptions}.

\subsection{Audit Hypotheses}
We state three falsifiable hypotheses in advance of the empirical sections below (\S\ref{sec:synthetic}--\S\ref{sec:audit}). We do \emph{not} number a fourth hypothesis for ``$0$D persistence is structurally equivalent to the MST'': that claim is Theorem~\ref{thm:mst} (\S\ref{sec:method}), a mathematical identity settled by proof, not an empirical question we test.
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\mathbf{H^{(1)}}$ \textbf{(Mechanical Simplicial Scaling):} Total $1$D cyclic persistence $P_1(N)$ tends to increase with sequence length $N$ due to clique expansion (a mechanical tendency, not a proven monotonicity -- Appendix~\ref{app:prop2_proof}). \emph{Verdict (\S\ref{sec:audit}, Appendix~\ref{app:scaling}):} \textbf{supported} -- Spearman $r=0.32$--$0.68$ between $P_1$ and $N$ across all five settings, all $p<10^{-10}$ (Table~\ref{tab:scaling_regression}); we do not claim this establishes a universal rate (see Conjecture~\ref{prop:scaling}'s honest hedge on the exponent).
    \item $\mathbf{H^{(2)}}$ \textbf{(Negligible Incremental Information of $H_1$):} Conditioned on $0$D persistence and sequence length $N$, length-normalized $1$D persistence carries no practically useful incremental information about hallucination label $Y$. \emph{Verdict:} \textbf{supported on TruthfulQA} (equivalence confirmed at every margin for \texttt{Mistral-7B}, at $\epsilon{=}0.025$ for \texttt{SmolLM-1.7B}; underpowered/inconclusive for \texttt{Qwen2.5-3B}, \texttt{Phi-3-mini}), \textbf{not supported on HaluEval} when conditioned on length alone (95\% CI excludes zero, $+0.010$ to $+0.016$ AUC), but \textbf{fully supported under joint length and lexical conditioning} ($\Delta\text{AUC} \le 0.000$).
    \item $\mathbf{H^{(3)}}$ \textbf{(Observational Equivalence of $0$D to Marginals):} $0$D persistence is observationally equivalent to non-topological attention statistics within margin $\epsilon = 0.015$. \emph{Verdict (\S\ref{sec:method}, Appendix~\ref{app:prop4_corr}):} \textbf{not supported as a general claim} -- correlation with peak row-attention is strong on three settings ($r\ge0.80$) but weak on \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B} ($r=0.21$) despite that setting having the paper's highest $0$D AUC ($0.904$); $0$D also exceeds the best available comparator column on 5 of 7 audited settings (Table~\ref{tab:main_results}) -- and exceeds the \emph{strictly} non-topological baselines (Length, Maximum Softmax Probability [MSP]) in every setting where those were extracted.
\end{itemize}

\section{Mathematical Characterization, and an Exact, Faster Replacement for 0D TDA}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Characterization: Classical Equivalence between 0D Persistence and Kruskal's MST}
We first formalize the exact mathematical nature of $0$D attention persistence.

\begin{theorem}[Equivalence to Kruskal's MST \citep{gower1969, edelsbrunner2010, carlsson2009}]
\label{thm:mst}
Let $G = (V, E, D)$ be a complete edge-weighted graph on $N$ vertices with symmetric weights $D_{ij} = 1 - \max(A_{ij}, A_{ji})$ and $D_{ii} = 0$. The set of finite death values $\{d_i\}_{i=1}^{N-1}$ in the $0$-dimensional Vietoris--Rips persistent homology barcode of $G$ is identical as a multiset to the edge weights of the Minimum Spanning Tree $\text{MST}(G)$ computed via Kruskal's algorithm under single-linkage hierarchical clustering.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $e_1, e_2, \dots, e_{\binom{N}{2}}$ denote the edges of $G$ sorted in non-decreasing order of weight $w(e_k) = D_{u_k, v_k}$. In the Vietoris--Rips filtration, all $N$ vertices are born at scale $\epsilon = 0$. When edge $e_k = (u_k, v_k)$ is added at scale $\epsilon = w(e_k)$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item If $u_k$ and $v_k$ belong to distinct connected components $C_A$ and $C_B$, adding $e_k$ merges them into $C_A \cup C_B$. By the Elder Rule of persistent homology, the younger component dies at scale $d = w(e_k)$. In Kruskal's algorithm, exactly when $u_k$ and $v_k$ belong to distinct trees, $e_k$ is added to the spanning forest.
    \item If $u_k$ and $v_k$ belong to the same component, no $0$D class dies (instead creating a 1-cycle or filling a higher simplex). In Kruskal's algorithm, $e_k$ forms a cycle and is discarded.
\end{enumerate}
Since $G$ is finite and connected for $\epsilon \ge \max D_{uv}$, exactly $N-1$ edges merge distinct components. Thus, the finite death values $\{d_i\}_{i=1}^{N-1}$ are identically the MST edge weights $\{w(e) : e \in \text{MST}(G)\}$. Summing lifetimes yields:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Total Persistence}(H_0) = \sum_{i=1}^{N-1} (d_i - 0) = \sum_{e \in \text{MST}(G)} w(e) = \text{weight}(\text{MST}(G)).
\end{equation}
The complete inductive proof is provided in Appendix \ref{app:proof}. This multiset identity is classical in algebraic topology \citep{gower1969, edelsbrunner2010}; its consequence for attention-graph interpretability -- that $0$D TDA computes Kruskal's MST and can therefore be replaced by it exactly -- is what \S\ref{sec:mst-tda} exploits.
\end{proof}

\subsection{MST-TDA: An Exact, Drop-in, Order-of-Magnitude-Faster Extractor}
\label{sec:mst-tda}
Theorem~\ref{thm:mst} is not merely descriptive: it licenses replacing every downstream use of $0$D Ripser persistence with a direct Kruskal MST computation, with \emph{zero} approximation error, since the two are the same number by construction rather than by empirical similarity. We package this as \textbf{MST-TDA}, a $\sim\!50$-line drop-in feature extractor: symmetrize $A^{(l)}$, form $D^{(l)}$, call \texttt{scipy.sparse.csgraph.minimum\_spanning\_tree}, and use the resulting edge-weight sum ($f_1 = \sum_{e\in\text{MST}} w(e)$) and maximum MST edge weight ($f_2 = \max_{e\in\text{MST}} w(e)$, the max-lifetime analogue) as the per-layer descriptor -- no simplicial complex, no persistence diagram, and no dependence on a homology library at all. Because causal attention makes $A^{(l)}$ lower-triangular, the symmetrization $W^{(l)}=\max(A^{(l)},(A^{(l)})^T)$ simply mirrors the lower triangle into the upper one; it is a bookkeeping step, not a filtering choice with free parameters.

\paragraph{Numerical exactness and wall-clock cost.} We confirm the two quantities agree exactly (max.\ absolute discrepancy $<10^{-12}$ in float64) on real attention graphs across every layer of every held-out \texttt{Mistral-7B} \texttt{TruthfulQA} example ($52{,}288$ comparisons; expected from the proof, not a new finding, but ruling out library-level implementation discrepancies). Full complexity profiling (Appendix~\ref{app:baselines}, Table~\ref{tab:complexity}) across $N \in [32, 512]$ on dense random matrices (a conservative worst case) shows: (i) against \emph{full} $0$D+$1$D Ripser, MST-TDA is $13$--$32{,}600\times$ faster, with the gap widening as Ripser's $1$D reduction exhibits severe super-cubic blow-up ($177$s at $N{=}256 \to 21{,}502$s at $N{=}512$, an empirical rate of roughly $N^{6.9}$ between these two points); (ii) against \emph{$0$D Ripser alone}, MST-TDA offers \textbf{no speedup} ($13.15$ms vs.\ $9.31$ms at $N{=}64$, and MST is slower at every $N$ we test) -- unsurprising, since Ripser's own $0$D pass is already a union-find over sorted edges, i.e.\ Kruskal's algorithm, and Theorem~\ref{thm:mst} identifies MST-TDA's $0$D output with exactly that computation. The three-orders-of-magnitude saving this paper's title refers to is therefore a statement about the cost of computing $1$D homology, which our audit below finds unnecessary, not a claim that Kruskal's algorithm beats itself.

\paragraph{Recovering per-head signal -- but so does a non-topological statistic at the same resolution.} Head-averaging is often assumed to discard little, but on a balanced $N{=}400$ HaluEval QA subset, head-averaged $0$D achieves AUC $0.677$ while per-head MST-TDA (one MST per attention head, concatenated, $160$ dims) reaches AUC $\mathbf{0.892}$ -- adding $1$D on top gives no further gain (combined $0.868$). This accuracy gain is attributable to per-head \emph{resolution}, not to topology specifically: we re-ran the same $200$ questions with a matched-dimension ($160$-feature) per-head \emph{non-topological} bank, and per-head mean attention entropy alone reaches AUC $0.902$, statistically indistinguishable from per-head $0$D ($\Delta=-0.010$, 95\% CI $[-0.031,+0.010]$ includes zero), while per-head max-attention is significantly \emph{worse} ($\Delta=+0.031$, CI $[+0.008,+0.054]$ excludes zero, favoring topology). So the fair, matched-dimension conclusion is: per-head resolution recovers real signal head-averaging discards, but on this benchmark we cannot claim per-head topological features are better than every simple non-topological per-head statistic -- they beat one (max-attention) and tie another (entropy). Full protocol, sample size, and all four pairwise comparisons with CIs are in Appendix~\ref{app:perhead}. We recommend per-head \emph{resolution} (topological or not) over head-averaging; MST-TDA remains the right way to get the topological version of it, since it is exactly reproducible and -- because it skips $1$D homology -- three orders of magnitude cheaper than the full $0$D+$1$D pipeline it replaces.

\subsection{Conjecture 1: Mechanical Sequence-Length Scaling of 1D Cycles}
\begin{conjecture}[Mechanical Simplicial Scaling of 1D Persistence via Chordless Cycles]
\label{prop:scaling}
Let $G_N$ be a complete graph on $N$ vertices with edge weights $D_{ij}$ drawn i.i.d.\ from continuous distribution $F_D$ on $[0, 1]$. In the Vietoris--Rips complex $\text{VR}(G_N, \epsilon)$, non-trivial $1$-cycles with positive persistence lifetime ($\ell = d - b > 0$) are formed by induced chordless cycles of length $k \ge 4$. For minimal chordless $4$-cycles, the number of candidate 4-tuples is $\binom{N}{4} = \Theta(N^4)$, and, considered in isolation on its own 4 vertices, each forms an open $1$-cycle when its four perimeter edges appear before its two internal chords, with probability $1/15$ and positive conditional expected lifetime under i.i.d.\ continuous weights. This isolated-tuple calculation does \emph{not} by itself establish monotonicity of the aggregate $\mathbb{E}[P_1(N)]$ in the full complex, because additional vertices can also \emph{shorten} existing cycles' lifetimes via coning (Appendix~\ref{app:prop2_proof}, Step 4). We therefore do \emph{not} prove that $\mathbb{E}[P_1(N)]$ is non-decreasing or state a proven growth rate, and give the following only as an empirically-supported conjecture:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}[P_1(N)] = \Omega(N) \quad \textbf{(conjectured; not proven below).}
\end{equation}
\end{conjecture}

\begin{proof}[Argument, and why the $\Omega(N)$ rate is a conjecture, not a corollary]
3-cycles have zero lifetime in a Vietoris--Rips complex (their 2-simplex enters at the same scale as their last edge). Non-trivial $1$-cycles arise from chordless $4$-cycles: in isolation, a 4-tuple's cycle is born when its four perimeter edges have all entered and dies when the first of its two diagonal chords enters, giving lifetime $\ell = \max(0, \min(C_1,C_2) - \max(E_1,..,E_4)) > 0$ with probability $1/15$ under i.i.d.\ continuous weights (uniform case: $\Pr = 1/15$, conditional expected lifetime $1/7$), so each of the $\Theta(N^4)$ candidate 4-tuples has positive \emph{marginal} expected lifetime when considered on its own 4 vertices. This does \emph{not} immediately give $\mathbb{E}[P_1(N)]$ non-decreasing in the full $N$-vertex complex, let alone a rate: adding a $(N{+}1)$-th vertex $v$ can also \emph{kill} an existing 4-tuple's cycle earlier than either of its own two chords would, if the four triangles $(v,v_i,v_{i+1})$ coning over the cycle all enter before both diagonal chords -- that cone's boundary is exactly the 4-cycle, so it can fill the class independently of the chords considered in isolation. We therefore do not claim a proof of monotonicity or of any specific rate; we verified the $1/15$, $1/7$ marginal-lifetime calculation above, and treat $\mathbb{E}[P_1(N)]=\Omega(N)$ as a conjecture, checked empirically both under the Proposition's own i.i.d.\ model ($\hat\alpha\approx1.61$ simulated) and on real attention graphs across five settings ($\hat\alpha \in [0.16,1.40]$ whole-dataset fit, Table~\ref{tab:scaling_regression}). Full argument, the cone-killing caveat, and both empirical checks are in Appendix~\ref{app:prop2_proof}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proposition 2: Length Normalization Removes a Length-Mediated Shortcut}
\begin{proposition}[Length-Invariant Normalization]
\label{prop:dag}
Let $S \in \{0, 1\}$ denote the latent truthfulness state, $N \in \mathbb{N}$ sequence length, $Z \in \mathbb{R}$ raw $1$D topological persistence, and $Y \in \{0, 1\}$ the observed label, with $Y=S$. This is a predictive-shortcut-removal statement, not a causal identification result: we are not estimating a causal effect, only constructing a feature that does not use $N$ as a proxy for $S$. Assume the structural model
\begin{equation}
    Z = g(N) \cdot (\psi(S) + \eta), \quad \eta \perp (N, S), \quad N \sim P(N \mid S),
\end{equation}
i.e.\ length rescales a signal-plus-noise term multiplicatively (this multiplicative-noise assumption is what makes the normalization exact; additive noise independent of $N$ would not cancel). Under power-law length scaling $g(N) = c \cdot N^{\hat{\alpha}}$, the normalized statistic $Z^\star = Z / N^{\hat{\alpha}}$ satisfies $Z^\star \perp N \mid S$: it no longer carries the length-mediated shortcut $Z \leftarrow N \leftarrow S \equiv Y$ that a classifier could otherwise exploit instead of learning $\psi(S)$. The exponent $\hat{\alpha}$ must be estimated empirically (see Section~\ref{sec:prop3_verification}).
\end{proposition}

\subsubsection{Empirical Verification of Scaling Exponent}
\label{sec:prop3_verification}
Fitting $\hat{\alpha}$ (via $\log P_1 = \hat{\alpha}\log N+\hat\beta$) on the whole dataset (Table~\ref{tab:scaling_regression}, $\hat\alpha \in [0.16,1.40]$) risks a mild leak into the very normalization it constructs, so we additionally report a \textbf{leakage-free, per-fold} estimate -- fit by OLS on each grouped CV split's training rows only -- yielding systematically lower exponents ($\hat\alpha \in [0.09, 0.45]$, Appendix~\ref{app:scaling}) but the same qualitative story: no dataset with a usable fit is near the default $Z/N$ assumption of $1.0$. Substituting this calibrated normalization for default $Z/N$ is \emph{not} inconsequential, and we correct an earlier version of this claim here: it leaves both HaluEval $\Delta$AUCs essentially unchanged ($\le 0.0003$), but moves three TruthfulQA settings by $0.0037$--$0.0086$ and flips two TOST verdicts in opposite directions (\texttt{SmolLM-1.7B} \emph{gains} equivalence at $\epsilon{=}0.020$; \texttt{TinyLlama-1.1B} \emph{loses} it at $\epsilon{=}0.025$). We therefore report both normalizations side by side (Table~\ref{tab:alpha_vs_zn}) rather than claiming invariance: at $150$--$500$ groups the choice of normalization exponent is itself a researcher degree of freedom that should be pre-specified.

\subsection{Empirical Observation: 0D Persistence Tracks Peak Row-Attention}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:mst}, $P_0 = \text{weight}(\text{MST}(G)) \le N-1$; we do not claim a formal lower bound in terms of $\bar{W}_{\max} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_i \max_j A_{ij}$, the mean per-token maximum attention weight (this would require MST-selected edges to concentrate near each node's highest-weight edge, which need not hold in general), so we verify the relationship empirically instead. We call $\bar{W}_{\max}$ ``peak row-attention'' rather than ``sink mass'': it is the mean row-max of $A$, which is not necessarily concentrated on a dedicated sink/BOS column \citep{xiao2023efficient}, and we did not verify that it is. Spearman correlation between total $0$D persistence and $\bar{W}_{\max}$ reaches $r=0.84$ on HaluEval and $r=0.80$ on TruthfulQA (both \texttt{Qwen2.5-3B}, $p<10^{-60}$), but only $r=0.21$ on HaluEval (\texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B}) despite that setting's $0$D AUC being the highest in the paper ($0.904$, Table~\ref{tab:main_results}) -- so this observation, and the broader claim that $0$D TDA ``reduces to'' non-topological attention statistics, holds unevenly across models and is refuted as a general statement by this specific setting (full table Appendix~\ref{app:prop4_corr}).

\section{Synthetic Causal Ground-Truth Validation}
\label{sec:synthetic}
To rule out that the pipeline found no incremental $1$D signal merely because topological extraction is insensitive, we design a synthetic ground-truth benchmark across six regimes: \textbf{S1 (Pure Length Confound, tree-metric background)} -- identical near-1D-line ``tree metric'' distributions across classes (essentially zero true cycles by construction, regardless of $N$) except sequence length ($N\in[20,35]$ vs.\ $[55,80]$); \textbf{S1b (Pure Length Confound, i.i.d.\ background)} -- the same length split, but on i.i.d.\ Uniform$[0,1]$ edge weights, i.e.\ Conjecture~\ref{prop:scaling}'s own null model, which \emph{does} have a length-growing population of chordless cycles; \textbf{S2 (Pure Topology / Positive Control)} -- matched $N{=}40$, Class 1 has a planted $1$D cyclic cavity; \textbf{S3 (Pure Attention Marginals)} -- matched $N{=}40$, Class 1 has shifted entropy/sink density with no cycles; \textbf{S4 (Topology + Length)} -- both planted cycles and longer sequences; \textbf{S5 (Negative Control)} -- random matrices with permuted labels. S1 and S1b together test two different questions: S1 asks whether length \emph{alone}, absent any mechanical cycle-growth process, can fool $1$D persistence (it cannot); S1b asks whether the pipeline detects the length-driven clique-expansion mechanism Conjecture~\ref{prop:scaling} actually describes (it does, decisively).

\section{LLM Empirical Audit and Equivalence Testing}
\label{sec:audit}

\subsection{Multi-Model, Multi-Dataset Audited Evaluation}
Following the audit pipeline of Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}, we evaluate the full per-layer topological feature banks ($d=28$--$216$) across five open-weight model families at six model scales spanning $1.1$B--$7$B parameters (\texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B}, \texttt{Qwen2.5-3B}, \texttt{SmolLM-1.7B}, \texttt{Phi-3-mini-3.8B}, \texttt{Mistral-7B}, \texttt{TinyLlama-1.1B}) on \texttt{HaluEval QA} \citep{li2024halueval} and \texttt{TruthfulQA} \citep{lin2022truthfulqa} under 10-fold grouped cross-validation (\texttt{StratifiedGroupKFold}). \textbf{Coverage caveat:} \texttt{HaluEval QA} is audited only on the two \texttt{Qwen2.5} scales ($N{=}4000$ and $N{=}1000$); \texttt{TruthfulQA} is audited on five of the six model instances -- \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B} is evaluated on \texttt{HaluEval QA} only -- at different sample sizes per model ($N_{\text{rows}}{=}300$ for \texttt{Qwen2.5-3B}/\texttt{Phi-3-mini}/\texttt{TinyLlama-1.1B}, $N_{\text{rows}}{=}1000$ for \texttt{SmolLM-1.7B}, $N_{\text{rows}}{=}1634$ for \texttt{Mistral-7B}). Throughout, $N_{\text{rows}}$ counts evaluated answer rows and $N_{\text{groups}}$ counts question groups (two rows per group); the two are listed per setting in Tables~\ref{tab:main_results_full} and~\ref{tab:power}. Cross-model comparisons in this section are therefore confounded with sample size and should be read within-model (e.g.\ TOST power, Table~\ref{tab:power}), not as head-to-head architecture comparisons at matched $N$. \textbf{Prompt protocol.} Each model's attention is extracted under that model's own native instruction format rather than a single shared template: \texttt{[INST]\,\ldots\,[/INST]} for \texttt{Mistral-7B}, \texttt{<|user|>\ldots<|end|>} for \texttt{Phi-3-mini}, \texttt{<|user|>\ldots</s>} for \texttt{TinyLlama-1.1B}, ChatML for \texttt{SmolLM-1.7B}, and \texttt{apply\_chat\_template} for \texttt{Qwen2.5-3B}. Prompting each model as it expects is the more faithful choice, but it is not a uniform protocol, and one asymmetry is worth naming: \texttt{Qwen2.5-3B} alone receives a system turn (\texttt{You are a helpful assistant.}), which lengthens its sequences by roughly ten tokens relative to the other four. Cross-model comparisons of any length-sensitive quantity should be read with this in mind as well as with the sample-size caveat above. \texttt{Mistral-7B} is run via int8 weight-only CPU quantization (our original 16GB-RAM evaluation hardware could not reliably hold a 14GB fp16 7B model), which also incidentally provided the real-attention-graph data for the Theorem~\ref{thm:mst} exactness check in \S\ref{sec:mst-tda}; we have since verified on a consumer GPU that native-precision extraction reproduces the int8 features exactly (see the degeneracy diagnostic below), so no result in this paper depends on that quantization. All numbers below use one canonical pipeline throughout the paper -- \texttt{StandardScaler} fitted strictly within training folds, followed by $L_2$-regularized logistic regression ($C{=}1.0$, \texttt{lbfgs}, up to $2000$ iterations) -- matching the protocol stated in Appendix~\ref{app:hyperparams}. We flag this explicitly because an earlier internal version of this evaluation fit logistic regression on \emph{unscaled} persistence features for some tables and scaled features for others; unscaled fitting silently suppresses the numerically smaller length-normalized $1$D features relative to $0$D under $L_2$ regularization, making the two banks look nearly identical and yielding an artificially tight, near-zero $\Delta$AUC with implausibly narrow bootstrap confidence intervals. That inconsistency has been resolved by standardizing on the scaled pipeline everywhere (code released); the numbers reported here and in Table~\ref{tab:equivalence} reflect a single evaluation protocol used to generate the entire paper.

\textbf{Diagnosing the \texttt{Mistral-7B} $1$D degeneracy.} Only $4$ of $1{,}634$ rows ($0.24\%$) have any non-zero $1$D feature at all, versus $99.7$--$100\%$ of rows in every other \texttt{TruthfulQA} setting. We are able to rule out two candidate explanations and to identify a third that holds across all five settings (Table~\ref{tab:degeneracy}).

\emph{Quantization is not the cause.} We re-extracted the first $100$ questions at native \texttt{bfloat16} precision on a consumer GPU, under the identical prompt protocol, and reproduced the int8 result exactly: $100.00\%$ of $1$D cells zero in both runs, $0$ of $200$ rows with any non-zero $1$D feature in both, and an identical mean sequence length of $32.47$. An earlier version of this paper left this unverified because the comparison exceeded its evaluation hardware; it is now settled, and the degeneracy is a property of the model and protocol rather than of the int8 weights.

\emph{Sequence length is not the cause either.} An earlier version attributed the degeneracy partly to \texttt{Mistral-7B}'s shorter sequences ($\bar N{=}33.0$) relative to \texttt{Qwen2.5-3B} ($\bar N{=}42.9$) through the $\Theta(N^4)$ candidate count of Conjecture~\ref{prop:scaling}. That explanation does not survive comparison with the remaining settings: \texttt{Phi-3-mini} ($\bar N{=}27.8$) and \texttt{SmolLM-1.7B} ($\bar N{=}32.2$) have shorter or comparable sequences and yet $99.7\%$ and $100\%$ of their rows respectively carry non-zero $1$D features. \texttt{Mistral-7B} is only the third-shortest of the five settings, so length alone cannot separate it from the others, and we withdraw that factor.

\emph{What does track the degeneracy is attention concentration.} Ordering the five settings by mean length-normalized $0$D total persistence $\overline{P_0}/N$ -- lower values indicating more concentrated, higher-weight attention -- rank-orders their $1$D zero-rates almost exactly (Spearman $\rho=0.90$; with only five settings we report this as a consistent descriptive pattern, $p\approx0.08$, not as a significance test). \texttt{Mistral-7B} sits at the extreme of that ordering ($\overline{P_0}/N=0.238$). Mechanically this is the factor Conjecture~\ref{prop:scaling} would predict to matter: concentrated attention makes the specific edge ordering a persistent $1$-cycle requires -- four large-distance perimeter edges entering before either of two small-distance chords -- less likely to occur at any $N$.

\emph{The result is, however, prompt-sensitive.} Each extraction script uses its own model's native instruction format (see ``Prompt protocol'' above). Re-extracting \texttt{Mistral-7B} with the chat template used for \texttt{Qwen2.5-3B}, which prepends a system turn and raises $\bar N$ from $32.5$ to $42.5$, removes the degeneracy entirely: $100\%$ of rows then carry non-zero $1$D features. We therefore read \texttt{Mistral-7B}'s TOST-CONFIRMED-at-every-margin result as evidence about \emph{this model under this prompt protocol}, not as a general claim about $7$B-scale models, and we caution that the $\overline{P_0}/N$ regime a model occupies is itself partly a function of how it is prompted.

\subsection{A Second, Independent Confound: Surface-Form Separability in HaluEval QA}
\label{sec:lexical-confound}
The length confound is not the only shortcut available on HaluEval QA: each example pairs a grounded and a hallucinated answer generated by different processes (extracted vs.\ LLM-fabricated), so the two can differ in surface form alone. Using only the raw answer text (no question, no model internals), a logistic regression on \emph{character length alone} reaches AUC $0.975$, and TF-IDF on the answer text alone reaches AUC $0.962$ -- both surviving the benchmark's standard token-length matching ($0.974$/$0.960$), since that matching balances total token count, not lexical style. A weaker version appears on TruthfulQA (TF-IDF AUC $0.830$; length-only near chance, consistent with its length balance, Appendix~\ref{app:lengthstats}). Full results in Table~\ref{tab:lexical}, Appendix~\ref{app:lexical}.

\paragraph{Joint Confound Conditioning.} Crucially, we test whether the apparent small positive increment of length-normalized $1$D persistence on HaluEval QA ($+0.010$ to $+0.016$ AUC, Table~\ref{tab:equivalence}) is itself an artifact of this surface-form confound. We jointly condition $1$D persistence on sequence length, answer character length, word count, and top TF-IDF components under canonical grouped 10-fold CV. Without lexical controls, adding $H_1^\star$ to $(H_0, N)$ yields $\Delta\text{AUC} = +0.0149$ (\texttt{Qwen2.5-3B}) and $+0.0160$ (\texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B}). When controlling jointly for lexical surface-form features (which alone achieve AUC $0.977$), the base model achieves AUC $0.9801$, and adding $H_1^\star$ yields $\Delta\text{AUC} = -0.0002$ (\texttt{Qwen2.5-3B}) and $\Delta\text{AUC} = -0.0003$ (\texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B}). Thus, under rigorous joint confound conditioning, the apparent $1$D incremental topological signal on HaluEval collapses to zero ($\le 0.000$).

This is an independent confound from Conjecture~\ref{prop:scaling}'s length effect, applies to \emph{any} probe on this benchmark, and is consistent with concurrent audits of hallucination-detection benchmark construction \citep{hussain2026parallax, janiak2025illusion}. It qualifies every $0$D/$1$D AUC on HaluEval QA in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}: some share of the signal available to \emph{any} classifier here may reflect answer-generation style rather than truthfulness, and length-matching alone does not control for it. We know of no matching procedure that removes this confound, and flag it as a limitation of any conclusion drawn from this benchmark, ours included.

\subsection{Formal Equivalence Testing (Two One-Sided Tests - TOST)}
We implement TOST with 10,000 group-level cluster-bootstrap resamples, testing $H_0: |\Delta\text{AUC}|\ge\epsilon$ vs.\ $H_1: |\Delta\text{AUC}|<\epsilon$ (confirmed iff $p<0.05$), at a pre-specified primary margin $\epsilon=0.015$, with the three larger margins shown directly in Table~\ref{tab:equivalence} and the full four-margin sweep $\{0.010,0.015,0.020,0.025\}$ in Table~\ref{tab:epsilon_sensitivity}, since the conclusion is margin-dependent. \textbf{At the pre-specified margin, equivalence is confirmed for exactly one of the seven settings} -- \texttt{Mistral-7B}, where the $1$D bank is degenerate rather than merely uninformative -- so the honest reading is not ``$1$D adds nothing'' but ``$1$D adds a small, sometimes real effect not negligible enough to call equivalent at our strictest margin.'' HaluEval's two settings have $95\%$ CIs excluding zero ($+0.010$ to $+0.016$ AUC), reaching equivalence only at $\epsilon\ge0.020$--$0.025$. Of the five TruthfulQA settings, all but \texttt{Mistral-7B} have CIs including zero; \texttt{SmolLM-1.7B} and \texttt{TinyLlama-1.1B} reach equivalence only at $\epsilon{=}0.025$, while \texttt{Qwen2.5-3B} and \texttt{Phi-3-mini} reach it at no margin -- but with only $150$--$500$ groups these four are underpowered (\S\ref{sec:power}), not confident nulls. Substituting leakage-free $\hat\alpha$-calibrated normalization for default $Z/N$ changes the HaluEval $\Delta$AUCs by $\le 0.0003$ but changes three TruthfulQA $\Delta$AUCs by up to $0.0086$ and flips two equivalence verdicts (Table~\ref{tab:alpha_vs_zn}). An independent corroboration by a log-loss information measure rather than an AUC-based test is given in Appendix~\ref{app:mi}, and worked qualitative examples of both confound mechanisms in Appendix~\ref{app:cases}; a layerwise depth breakdown is in Appendix~\ref{app:layerwise}. Our TOST $p$-values are percentile-bootstrap tail masses, which coincide with exact TOST $p$-values only when the bootstrap distribution of $\Delta$AUC is symmetric -- an assumption that is not free at $150$ groups. We therefore re-derived every verdict from a bias-corrected bootstrap interval and find the distributions are close to symmetric in all seven settings ($|\text{skew}|\le0.08$, $|z_0|\le0.04$), with \emph{no} verdict changing at any of the four margins (Appendix~\ref{app:bca}, Table~\ref{tab:bca}).

\subsubsection{Power Analysis and Minimum Detectable Effect}
\label{sec:power}
A null result is only informative if well-powered: at $80\%$ power, $\alpha=0.05$, MDE$=2.802\hat\sigma$ (Appendix~\ref{app:power}), HaluEval ($N_{\text{groups}}\in\{500,2000\}$) achieves $>84\%$ power to detect $\Delta\text{AUC}=0.015$, while TruthfulQA ($N_{\text{groups}}=150$) has only $14$--$43\%$. HaluEval's non-equivalence is thus well-powered and genuine; TruthfulQA's is underpowered and inconclusive rather than evidence of a real effect.


\section{Discussion, Recommendations, and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}
Our findings recast, rather than merely debunk, attention-graph TDA for hallucination detection. Its $0$-dimensional component is mathematically equivalent to Kruskal's Minimum Spanning Tree (Theorem~\ref{thm:mst}) and can be computed directly at three orders of magnitude lower cost with no loss of accuracy (\S\ref{sec:mst-tda}) -- and, extended per-head, at \emph{higher} accuracy than the status quo ($0.892$ vs.\ $0.677$ AUC). Its $1$-dimensional component is heavily confounded by sequence length (Conjecture~\ref{prop:scaling}) and contributes a real but small increment under formal equivalence testing ($+0.010$ to $+0.016$ AUC on well-powered settings, Table~\ref{tab:equivalence}) that does not, in our judgment, justify its cost at any model scale we tested. A second, independent surface-form confound in HaluEval QA (\S\ref{sec:lexical-confound}) further qualifies what any probe -- topological or otherwise -- can be said to have learned on that specific benchmark.

\paragraph{What Does Work: Token Log-Probability vs.\ 0D/MST across Benchmarks.} In our initial diagnostic on HaluEval QA (Appendix~\ref{app:hs_probe}), a logistic regression on the model's own mean/min per-token log-probability reached AUC $\mathbf{0.984}$, outperforming all topological and hidden-state probes. However, full-scale evaluation across all seven settings at full $N$ (Appendix~\ref{app:mst_vs_logprob}, Table~\ref{tab:mst_vs_logprob}) reveals a critical benchmark divergence:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{On HaluEval QA (lexically contaminated):} token log-probability dominates ($0.981$ vs.\ $0.808$ on \texttt{Qwen2.5-3B}; $0.990$ vs.\ $0.878$ on \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B}), reflecting that fabricated text is locally less predictable and entangled with the surface-form confound of \S\ref{sec:lexical-confound}.
    \item \textbf{On TruthfulQA (length-balanced, full $N{=}817$):} the reverse holds -- \textbf{0D/MST significantly outperforms token log-probability on 4 of 5 models} by $+0.077$ to $+0.193$ AUC, with all $95\%$ bootstrap CIs strictly excluding zero (\texttt{Qwen2.5-3B}: $0.760$ vs.\ $0.567$, $\Delta=+0.193$; \texttt{Phi-3-mini}: $0.652$ vs.\ $0.529$, $\Delta=+0.123$; \texttt{Mistral-7B}: $0.685$ vs.\ $0.607$, $\Delta=+0.078$; \texttt{TinyLlama-1.1B}: $0.654$ vs.\ $0.578$, $\Delta=+0.077$; \texttt{SmolLM-1.7B}: tie at $0.579$ vs.\ $0.576$).
    \item \textbf{Complementary signal:} Combining 0D/MST with token log-probability improves AUC across every setting (e.g.\ reaching $0.767$ on \texttt{Qwen2.5-3B}, $0.713$ on \texttt{Mistral-7B}, and $0.677$ on \texttt{TinyLlama-1.1B}).
\end{itemize}
Furthermore, while $0$D on HaluEval is absorbed by lexical features, on TruthfulQA $0$D/MST \textbf{survives strong 50- and 200-component TF-IDF lexical controls} (Appendix~\ref{app:lex_survival}, Table~\ref{tab:lex_survival}), adding $+0.013$ to $+0.058$ incremental AUC over bag-of-words (all $95\%$ CIs exclude zero). Thus, on length-balanced benchmarks, $0$D/MST captures genuine internal model signal that simple surface-form and token-probability baselines miss.

\paragraph{Recommendations:} (1) Use MST-TDA, not Ripser -- it removes the homology-library dependency and, by skipping $1$D, is $1{,}200\times$ cheaper than the full $0$D+$1$D pipeline (though no faster than Ripser's own $0$D pass, which computes the same MST); we release our implementation. (2) Benchmark sensitivity with positive/negative controls (our S1--S5 suite, \S\ref{sec:synthetic}) before publishing a topological probe. (3) Normalize with a leakage-free, per-fold $\hat\alpha$ (\S\ref{sec:prop3_verification}), not default $Z/N$. (4) Report equivalence at a pre-specified margin with a power analysis and full margin sensitivity, not just significance (Table~\ref{tab:equivalence}, Appendix~\ref{app:power}). (5) Audit the benchmark, not only the method, for length and lexical confounds (\S\ref{sec:lexical-confound}). (6) Note that for causal attention graphs, directed flag complexes collapse identically to symmetrized Vietoris--Rips complexes (Appendix~\ref{app:directed_collapse}), settling the directed-vs-symmetrized question for causal decoders.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} Building a hallucination detector from attention graphs today: replace Ripser-based $0$D+$1$D extraction with MST-TDA (provably identical to $0$D, no homology library, and $1{,}200\times$ cheaper than the full pipeline because it drops $1$D), extend it per-head, and skip $1$D cyclic persistence unless your application can absorb that compute premium for an AUC gain not established at practical equivalence margins on most of the settings we test. More broadly: before crediting a new mathematical abstraction with predictive power, check whether it is secretly something already understood, and audit whether the benchmark itself, not only the method, contains the signal being credited to it.

\section*{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Model Scale:} $1.1$B--$7$B parameters across five model families (\texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B}, \texttt{SmolLM-1.7B}, \texttt{Phi-3-mini-3.8B}, \texttt{Mistral-7B}, \texttt{TinyLlama-1.1B}); the $7$B model used int8 CPU quantization, which does not affect Theorem~\ref{thm:mst}'s identity (verified exactly on this model's real attention graphs, \S\ref{sec:mst-tda}). An earlier version of this paper could not rule out an int8-specific contribution to that model's $1$D degeneracy; we have since re-extracted at native \texttt{bfloat16} precision and reproduced the int8 features exactly, so the quantization is not load-bearing for any reported result (\S\ref{sec:audit}). Future work should audit $70$B+ models at native precision.
    \item \textbf{Teacher-Forced, Not On-Policy -- a Construct-Validity Limit, Not Just a Distributional One:} Every example is a teacher-forced pass over a dataset-provided grounded answer and a dataset-provided (extracted or LLM-fabricated) incorrect answer, never the audited model's \emph{own} generation. The task we measure is therefore ``does this model's attention distinguish dataset-provided true text from dataset-provided false text,'' which is the setting most exposed to the surface-form confound in \S\ref{sec:lexical-confound}, not ``does this model's attention flag its own hallucinations.'' We did not add an on-policy benchmark (model-generated answers graded against gold, as in \citealp{farquhar2024semantic}) in this revision; this is a real gap, not merely a stylistic one, and every AUC in this paper should be read with that caveat. Evaluations also use static symmetrized head-averaged matrices; \S\ref{sec:mst-tda}/Appendix~\ref{app:perhead} show per-head resolution recovers additional signal (though not decisively more than a matched-dimension non-topological per-head statistic, Appendix~\ref{app:perhead}).
    \item \textbf{HaluEval Grouping and Surface-Form Confound:} Grouped CV keeps each question's grounded/hallucinated pair in one fold ($\sim$100 groups/test-fold), preventing semantic leakage -- but (\S\ref{sec:lexical-confound}) HaluEval's answers are separable by length or bag-of-words alone at AUC $0.96$--$0.98$ even after length matching, affecting every AUC on this benchmark (TDA and hidden-state alike), though not the \emph{relative} comparisons between feature banks that are our main claims, since all banks share the same confound.
    \item \textbf{Baseline Coverage and Topological Family Scope:} We do not audit Lookback Lens \citep{chuang2024lookback}, LLM-Check \citep{sriramanan2024llmcheck}, EigenScore/INSIDE \citep{chen2024inside}, or semantic-entropy probes \citep{kossen2024semantic} on our splits. We have, however, comprehensively evaluated the token log-probability baseline and lexical controls at full $N$ across all seven settings (Appendices~\ref{app:lex_survival} and~\ref{app:mst_vs_logprob}). Both of our TOHA MTop-Div variants are themselves MST statistics, differing from MST-TDA only in node contractions. This paper therefore focuses on the MST/persistent-homology family Theorem~\ref{thm:mst} characterizes. We verified numerically that for causal autoregressive decoders, directed flag complexes collapse identically to symmetrized Vietoris--Rips complexes (Appendix~\ref{app:directed_collapse}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Additional Formal Assumptions}
\label{app:assumptions}
\S\ref{sec:setup} states the two load-bearing assumptions of our audit (symmetric graph reduction, grouped evaluation partitions). Three further technical assumptions, standard throughout this literature, complete the formal setup:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Teacher-Forced Forward Pass:} Token representations and attention matrices are extracted in a single teacher-forced pass over concatenated prompt-response sequences $X = [X_{\text{prompt}}, X_{\text{response}}]$.
    \item \textbf{Monotonic Inversion:} Edge distances $D_{ij} = 1 - W_{ij}$ monotonically invert attention strength, forming an edge-weighted complete graph $(V, E, D)$.
    \item \textbf{Single-Linkage Component Merge:} $0$-dimensional persistence merges components along minimum pairwise edge distances according to the standard Elder rule.
\end{itemize}

\section{Formal Mathematical Proof of Theorem 1}
\label{app:proof}
\paragraph{Theorem 1 Restatement:} \textit{Let $G = (V, E, D)$ be a complete edge-weighted graph on $N$ vertices with symmetric weights $D_{ij} = 1 - \max(A_{ij}, A_{ji})$ and $D_{ii} = 0$. The set of finite death values $\{d_i\}_{i=1}^{N-1}$ in the 0-dimensional Vietoris--Rips persistent homology barcode $\text{dgm}_0(\text{VR}(G))$ is identical as a multiset to the edge weights in the Minimum Spanning Tree $\text{MST}(G)$ computed via Kruskal's algorithm under single-linkage hierarchical clustering.}

\paragraph{Complete Inductive Proof.}
Let $V = \{v_1, \dots, v_N\}$ denote the vertices of $G$, and let $E = \{e_1, \dots, e_M\}$ denote the complete set of $M = \binom{N}{2}$ undirected edges sorted in non-decreasing order of weight:
\begin{equation}
    w(e_1) \le w(e_2) \le \dots \le w(e_M), \quad \text{where } w(e_k) = D_{u_k, v_k}.
\end{equation}
In the Vietoris--Rips filtration $\text{VR}(G, \epsilon)$, the $0$-simplices are the vertices $V$, all born at filtration scale $\epsilon = 0$ ($b_i = 0$ for all $i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$). A 1-simplex (edge) $e = (u, v)$ enters the filtration at scale $\epsilon = w(e) = D_{uv}$.

Changes in the 0-dimensional homology group $H_0(\text{VR}(G, \epsilon))$ occur exclusively at the discrete values $\epsilon = w(e_k)$. When edge $e_k = (u_k, v_k)$ enters the complex:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Case 1 ($u_k$ and $v_k$ already belong to the same connected component):} The addition of edge $e_k$ creates a cycle in the 1-skeleton but leaves the number of connected components unchanged. Thus, no class in $H_0$ dies. In Kruskal's algorithm, edge $e_k$ forms a cycle in the spanning forest and is discarded.
    \item \textbf{Case 2 ($u_k$ and $v_k$ belong to distinct connected components $C_A$ and $C_B$):} The addition of edge $e_k$ merges $C_A$ and $C_B$ into a single component. By the Elder Rule of persistent homology, the younger component dies at scale $d = w(e_k)$. In Kruskal's algorithm, $e_k$ connects two disjoint trees and is added to the Minimum Spanning Tree.
\end{itemize}
Since $G$ is finite and connected for $\epsilon \ge \max D_{uv}$, exactly $N-1$ edges merge distinct components. Each such merge corresponds to an edge in $\text{MST}(G)$ and a finite death scale in $\text{dgm}_0(\text{VR}(G))$. The single remaining component persists indefinitely ($d_N = \infty$). Thus, $\{d_i\}_{i=1}^{N-1} \equiv \{w(e) : e \in \text{MST}(G)\}$. Summing lifetimes:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Total Persistence}(H_0) = \sum_{i=1}^{N-1} (d_i - b_i) = \sum_{i=1}^{N-1} d_i = \sum_{e \in \text{MST}(G)} w(e) = \text{weight}(\text{MST}(G)). \quad \blacksquare
\end{equation}

\section{Detailed Analysis of Conjecture 1 (Mechanical Simplicial Scaling)}
\label{app:prop2_proof}

\paragraph{Conjecture 1 Restatement:} \textit{Let $G_N$ be a complete graph on $N$ vertices with edge weights $D_{ij}$ drawn i.i.d.\ from continuous distribution $F_D$ on $[0,1]$. In the Vietoris--Rips complex $\mathrm{VR}(G_N, \epsilon)$, non-trivial $1$-cycles with positive persistence lifetime ($\ell = d - b > 0$) arise from induced chordless cycles of length $k \ge 4$. Each such 4-tuple has positive probability ($1/15$) and positive conditional expected lifetime ($1/7$) of contributing a persistent cycle when considered in isolation on its own 4 vertices (Steps 1--3 below); we conjecture, but do not prove, that this yields $\mathbb{E}[P_1(N)] = \Omega(N)$ in the full $N$-vertex complex (Step 4 explains why a full proof is harder than it looks: additional vertices can also kill existing cycles via coning).}

\paragraph{Step 1: Why 3-cycles have zero persistence lifetime in Vietoris--Rips complexes.}
In a Vietoris--Rips simplicial complex, a 2-simplex $\sigma = \{u, v, w\}$ enters the filtration at scale $\epsilon = \max(D_{uv}, D_{uw}, D_{vw})$. The boundary 1-cycle formed by the three edges $(u,v), (u,w), (v,w)$ is born at scale $b = \max(D_{uv}, D_{uw}, D_{vw})$ (the moment the last of the three edges enters). Since the 2-simplex $\sigma$ enters at this identical scale ($d = b$), the lifetime of every 3-cycle in a Vietoris--Rips complex is exactly zero ($d - b = 0$).

\paragraph{Step 2: Formation and death of minimal chordless 4-cycles.}
Non-trivial 1-cycles in $\mathrm{VR}(G_N, \cdot)$ require induced cycles of length at least 4 without diagonal chords. Consider any 4-tuple of vertices $V_4 = \{v_1, v_2, v_3, v_4\}$. There are 4 perimeter edges $E = \{(v_1, v_2), (v_2, v_3), (v_3, v_4), (v_4, v_1)\}$ with weights $e_1, e_2, e_3, e_4$, and 2 internal chords $C = \{(v_1, v_3), (v_2, v_4)\}$ with weights $c_1, c_2$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Birth scale $b$:} The 1-cycle around the 4-gon is formed when all 4 perimeter edges enter: $b = \max(e_1, e_2, e_3, e_4)$.
    \item \textbf{Death scale $d$:} The cycle dies when the first chord enters the complex: $d = \min(c_1, c_2)$. At scale $d$, the entering chord splits the 4-gon into two 3-cycles, each of which is immediately filled by a 2-simplex since all three of their boundary edges are already present ($\le d$).
\end{enumerate}
Thus, the 4-tuple contributes a persistent 1-cycle with lifetime:
\begin{equation}
    \ell(V_4) = \max\Big(0, \min(c_1, c_2) - \max(e_1, e_2, e_3, e_4)\Big).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Step 3: Positive expected lifetime under continuous edge distributions.}
For 6 independent draws from continuous distribution $F_D$, the 2 chords and 4 perimeter edges are exchangeable. By symmetry, the probability that both chords exceed all 4 perimeter edges is:
\begin{equation}
    \Pr\Big[\min(c_1, c_2) > \max(e_1, e_2, e_3, e_4)\Big] = \frac{4! \, 2!}{6!} = \frac{48}{720} = \frac{1}{15} \approx 0.0667.
\end{equation}
Conditioned on this event, the expected lifetime is strictly positive:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}\Big[\min(c_1, c_2) - \max(e_1, e_2, e_3, e_4) \;\Big|\; \min(c_1, c_2) > \max(e_1, \dots, e_4)\Big] = c_F > 0.
\end{equation}
For $F_D = \mathrm{Uniform}[0, 1]$, standard order-statistic integration yields $c_F = \frac{1}{7} \approx 0.143$, giving an unconditional expected lifetime $\mathbb{E}[\ell(V_4)] = \frac{1}{15} \cdot \frac{1}{7} = \frac{1}{105}$.

\paragraph{Step 4: Combinatorial scaling -- plausibility argument, not a full derivation.}
The number of candidate 4-tuples is $\binom{N}{4} = \frac{N(N-1)(N-2)(N-3)}{24} = \Theta(N^4)$, each contributing positive expected lifetime $\mathbb{E}[\ell(V_4)] = \frac{1}{105}$ (Step 3) with positive probability, \emph{when that 4-tuple is considered in isolation on its own 4 vertices}. If these marginal contributions to \emph{total} persistence were independent and additive, linearity of expectation would give $\mathbb{E}[P_1(N)] = \Theta(N^4)$ directly -- a far stronger claim than $\Omega(N)$. They are not independent, and the dependence is not merely a matter of overlapping edges attenuating a positive sum: when a $5$th vertex $w$ enters, the four triangles $(w,v_i,v_{i+1})$ coning over a 4-tuple's cycle can fill that cycle (the cone's boundary is exactly the 4-cycle) \emph{before either of the 4-tuple's own two diagonal chords enters}, which \emph{shortens} that cycle's lifetime relative to the isolated-4-tuple calculation in Step 3, rather than leaving it unaffected. Consequently, we do \textbf{not} claim that $\mathbb{E}[P_1(N)]$ is provably non-decreasing in $N$ (moving from $N$ to $N{+}1$ vertices can both add new candidate cycles and shorten or kill existing ones via this cone mechanism), and Step 1--3's calculation should be read only as establishing that a positive-probability, positive-expected-lifetime mechanism for creating $1$-cycles exists and its raw count of candidates grows as $\Theta(N^4)$ -- not as a proof of any monotonicity or rate for the aggregate $\mathbb{E}[P_1(N)]$. Results on random geometric and random clique complexes \citep{kahle2011, bobrowski2018} establish Betti-number growth regimes for related (but not identical) random complex models and are suggestive rather than a direct proof for the exact filtration studied here. We therefore treat both the non-decreasing-in-$N$ claim and any specific polynomial rate as an empirically-testable conjecture rather than a corollary of Steps 1--3, verified directly below and in Appendix~\ref{app:scaling}. $\blacksquare$

\paragraph{Remark (Direct Verification Under the Proposition's Own i.i.d.\ Model).}
To test the conjectured rate under the exact assumptions of the Proposition (rather than only on real, non-i.i.d.\ attention graphs), we simulate $D_{ij} \sim \mathrm{Uniform}[0,1]$, symmetrized, for $N \in \{16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128, 160, 200, 256\}$ ($8$ trials per $N$) and compute $P_1(N)$ via Ripser. The resulting curve is clean, monotone, and low-variance (e.g.\ $\mathbb{E}[P_1] \approx 1.84$ at $N{=}16$ rising to $\approx 168.0$ at $N{=}256$), and a log-log OLS fit gives $\hat\alpha \approx 1.61$ -- confirming super-linear growth holds even under the Proposition's literal null model, and if anything exceeds the conjectured $\Omega(N)$ rate (full data released with the code).

\paragraph{Remark (Real Attention Graphs vs.\ i.i.d.\ Models).}
Real transformer attention maps exhibit dense local attention clusters and attention sinks, which induce higher multi-token clique densities than independent random graphs. As a result, longer token sequences generate substantially more high-order cycle candidates, amplifying the length dependence. Our empirical regressions on five real model/dataset settings (Appendix~\ref{app:scaling}) confirm scaling exponents $\hat{\alpha} \in [0.16, 1.40]$ (whole-dataset fit) or $[0.09, 0.45]$ (leakage-free per-fold fit), demonstrating that unnormalized $1$D persistence is confounded by sequence length across every setting we audit, with the exact rate depending on the attention distribution of the specific model and dataset.

\section{Bootstrap Symmetry and Bias-Corrected Equivalence Verdicts}

\section{Hidden-State Probe Baseline}
\label{app:hs_probe}

To provide an empirical anchor for what attention-graph TDA performance means, we evaluate
hidden state probes from \texttt{Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct} on \texttt{HaluEval QA}
under the identical grouped cross-validation protocol. Hidden states encode richer information about
model beliefs than attention graphs, providing an upper-bound reference for the TDA signal.

We initially extracted three probe variants: (1) mean-pooled last-layer hidden states, (2) last-token
hidden states, and (3) PCA-compressed (64-dimensional) concatenation of first, middle, and last-layer
features. The last-token and PCA variants reached implausibly high AUC ($0.998$ and $0.997$
respectively) -- higher than any internal-representation hallucination probe we are aware of in the
literature on this task. We traced this to a construct-validity problem rather than a cross-validation
leak: our forward pass is teacher-forced over the \emph{full} prompt-plus-answer sequence, so the
last-token (and, through the PCA rotation, the all-layer) representation is dominated by the surface
identity of the final few answer tokens -- exactly the surface-form confound we quantify directly in
\S\ref{sec:lexical-confound}, where TF-IDF on the answer text \emph{alone} already reaches AUC $0.962$.
The last-token and PCA probes are therefore not construct-valid measures of an internal
``truthfulness'' representation on this benchmark, and we do not report them as such. We retain only
the \textbf{mean-pooled} last-layer probe, which pools evenly over the whole sequence and is
comparatively less dominated by the last few tokens' identity, as the anchor in Table~\ref{tab:hs_probe}.
A Logistic Regression with \texttt{StandardScaler} fitted strictly within training folds is used throughout.

\section{Head-Averaging Ablation: Protocol Detail}
\label{app:perhead}

\S\ref{sec:mst-tda} reports headline numbers for this ablation (head-averaged $0$D AUC $0.677$ vs.\ per-head MST-TDA concatenation AUC $0.892$); this appendix gives the full protocol, including a matched-dimension non-topological control the main text summarizes. A natural reviewer concern is that head-averaging discards head-specific topological structure -- and a second, equally natural concern is that any per-head statistic, topological or not, might recover comparable signal just from finer resolution. We test both. We re-extract per-head attention matrices for a balanced subset of HaluEval QA ($N=400$ examples, 200 questions) from \texttt{Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct}, focusing on mid-late layers 18--27 where head-averaged $0$D performance peaks (all $160$-dimensional feature banks below use the identical $10$ layers $\times$ $16$ heads grid on the identical $400$ rows, so every comparison is exactly matched in dimensionality and sample). For each layer--head pair we compute (a) $0$D and $1$D persistent homology, (b) mean per-token maximum attention weight (``max-attn''), and (c) mean per-token attention entropy. All four banks are evaluated with the same StandardScaler+LR, grouped 10-fold CV protocol as every other number in this paper, and every pairwise comparison uses a $5{,}000$-resample question-level cluster bootstrap (not a single TOST call at one margin) to report a $95\%$ CI on the difference:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Head-averaged 0D} (layers 18--27, 10 dims): AUC $= 0.677$ -- matches the main paper's mid-late layer 0D result on the same subset.
  \item \textbf{Per-head 0D concatenation} (160 dims): AUC $= 0.892$. vs.\ head-averaged: $\Delta=+0.214$, 95\% CI $[+0.188,+0.239]$ (excludes zero -- decisively not equivalent, favoring per-head resolution).
  \item \textbf{Per-head max-attention} (160 dims, matched): AUC $= 0.861$. vs.\ per-head 0D: $\Delta=+0.031$, 95\% CI $[+0.008,+0.054]$ (excludes zero -- per-head $0$D is significantly better than this particular non-topological statistic).
  \item \textbf{Per-head entropy} (160 dims, matched): AUC $= \mathbf{0.902}$ (nominally \emph{higher} than per-head $0$D). vs.\ per-head 0D: $\Delta=-0.010$, 95\% CI $[-0.031,+0.010]$ (includes zero -- \textbf{statistically indistinguishable} from per-head $0$D).
  \item \textbf{Per-head max-attn + entropy combined} (320 dims): AUC $= 0.888$, likewise statistically indistinguishable from per-head $0$D ($\Delta=+0.004$, CI $[-0.017,+0.025]$).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Honest reading:} per-head \emph{resolution} clearly beats head-averaging, for both topological and non-topological statistics. Whether per-head \emph{topology specifically} beats a matched-dimension non-topological alternative depends on which alternative: it beats max-attention, but ties entropy. We do not have a matched-dimension non-topological bank that per-head $0$D beats decisively, so the paper's claim that per-head MST-TDA is the best attention-graph descriptor should be read as ``a good and exactly-reproducible way to get per-head resolution,'' not as ``provably better than every non-topological per-head alternative.'' Adding $1$D cyclic persistence on top of per-head $0$D gives no further benefit (combined AUC $= 0.868$, slightly below per-head $0$D alone), and all per-head attention features remain below the mean-pooled hidden-state probe (AUC $0.903$, Appendix~\ref{app:hs_probe}). This ablation is run only on HaluEval QA (\texttt{Qwen2.5-3B}, $N{=}400$); we did not extend it to TruthfulQA or the other model families, which remains for future work.


\section{Conditional Predictive Information Gain (Permutation-Calibrated)}
\label{app:mi}

An earlier version of this appendix estimated $I(Y; H_1^\star \mid H_0, L)$ using the per-feature Kraskov $k$-nearest-neighbor mutual information estimator (\texttt{sklearn.feature\_selection.mutual\_info\_classif}), averaged across the $H_0$ feature bank's columns. That estimator is unsound for this use: (i) it computes mutual information \emph{per feature independently} and averages, which is not the joint multivariate conditional mutual information of a $72$--$216$-dimensional bank and systematically understates $I(Y;H_0)$ relative to what the bank's classification AUC ($0.62$--$0.90$) implies; and (ii) the conditioning step regresses $H_1^\star$ on $(H_0, L)$ by ordinary least squares on the \emph{full, unsplit} dataset before estimating mutual information on the residual, which overfits when the covariate dimension ($\mathrm{dim}(H_0)+1$) is large relative to $N$ (as low as $300$ for TruthfulQA), inflating the residual's apparent information content -- consistent with the previously reported values violating the expected inequality $I(Y; H_1^\star \mid H_0, L) \le I(Y; H_1^\star)$ in every row.

We replace it with a cross-validated \textbf{predictive information gain}: fit the canonical scaled logistic-regression pipeline (\S\ref{sec:audit}) once on $(H_0, L)$ alone and once on $(H_0, L, H_1^\star)$, both under the same grouped $10$-fold splits, and report $\hat{I} = \text{CV-LogLoss}(Y \mid H_0, L) - \text{CV-LogLoss}(Y \mid H_0, L, H_1^\star)$ in nats -- a standard, easily-interpreted lower bound on incremental predictive information that reuses the same classifier family as every AUC number in this paper. Because adding $216$ extra dimensions can \emph{mechanically} hurt held-out log-loss even when $H_1^\star$ carries zero true signal (pure overfitting cost), we calibrate $\hat{I}$ against a permutation null: we shuffle $Y$ and recompute $\hat{I}$ over $100$ permutations, which characterizes how negative $\hat{I}$ becomes from overfitting alone, then report a one-sided $p$-value for the observed $\hat{I}$ against that null.

\section{Equivalence of Directed Flag Complexes on Causal Attention Graphs}
\label{app:directed_collapse}

A common question regarding attention graph TDA is whether symmetrizing the attention matrix ($W = \max(A, A^T)$) discards topological information that could otherwise be captured by directed persistent homology, such as the filtered directed flag complex \citep{flagser2020}. Here we prove that on autoregressive causal attention graphs, directed flag complexes collapse identically to symmetrized Vietoris--Rips complexes.

\begin{theorem}[\textbf{Directed Flag Collapse under Causal Masking}]
\label{thm:directed_collapse}
Let $A \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ be a causal attention matrix satisfying strict lower-triangular support: $A_{ij} > 0 \iff j \le i$, with $A_{ij} = 0$ for all $j > i$. Let $\mathrm{DFl}(A)$ denote the filtered directed flag complex on the directed graph $G = (V, E, w)$ with vertex set $V = \{1, \dots, N\}$ and edge filtration weights $w(i \to j) = 1 - A_{ij}$ for $j \le i$. Let $\mathrm{VR}(W)$ denote the filtered Vietoris--Rips complex of the symmetrized distance matrix $D_{ij} = 1 - \max(A_{ij}, A_{ji})$ ($D_{ii} = 0$). Then $\mathrm{DFl}(A)$ and $\mathrm{VR}(W)$ are isomorphic as filtered simplicial complexes, and consequently have identical persistence diagrams in every homology dimension:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{dgm}_k(\mathrm{DFl}(A)) \equiv \mathrm{dgm}_k(\mathrm{VR}(W)), \quad \forall k \ge 0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof proceeds in two steps: structural simplicial equivalence and filtration weight identity.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Structural Simplex Bijection:} In a directed flag complex, a $k$-simplex is formed by an ordered $(k+1)$-tuple of vertices $(v_0, v_1, \dots, v_k)$ that forms a directed clique (a transitive tournament), meaning that the directed edge $v_a \to v_b$ exists for all $0 \le a < b \le k$. Because $A$ is strictly causal, directed edges $u \to v$ exist if and only if $u \ge v$. The natural index ordering on token indices $\{1, \dots, N\}$ is a strict total order. Therefore, for any subset of vertices $S \subseteq \{1, \dots, N\}$ of cardinality $k+1$, there is \emph{exactly one} permutation of $S$ whose elements are in strictly descending order ($v_0 > v_1 > \dots > v_k$). This unique ordering satisfies the directed clique condition, while all other $(k+1)! - 1$ permutations contain at least one forward edge $v_a \to v_b$ with $v_a < v_b$ which has weight $0$ (non-existent). Thus, the map sending each undirected simplex $S$ in $\mathrm{VR}(W)$ to the uniquely descending directed simplex in $\mathrm{DFl}(A)$ is a bijective simplicial isomorphism.
    \item \textbf{Filtration Identity:} The filtration value of the directed simplex $\sigma = (v_0, \dots, v_k)$ in $\mathrm{DFl}(A)$ is defined by the maximum weight of its constituent directed edges:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathrm{filt}_{\mathrm{DFl}}(\sigma) = \max_{0 \le a < b \le k} w(v_a \to v_b) = \max_{0 \le a < b \le k} (1 - A_{v_a, v_b}).
    \end{equation}
    In the symmetrized distance matrix $D$, for any pair $u > v$, we have $A_{vu} = 0$, which implies $D_{uv} = 1 - \max(A_{uv}, 0) = 1 - A_{uv}$. The filtration value of the undirected simplex $S = \{v_0, \dots, v_k\}$ in $\mathrm{VR}(W)$ is:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathrm{filt}_{\mathrm{VR}}(S) = \max_{u, v \in S, u > v} D_{uv} = \max_{0 \le a < b \le k} (1 - A_{v_a, v_b}).
    \end{equation}
    Because $\mathrm{filt}_{\mathrm{DFl}}(\sigma) = \mathrm{filt}_{\mathrm{VR}}(S)$ for every simplex, the filtration preserves the isomorphism at every filtration scale $\epsilon \ge 0$.
\end{enumerate}
Since the filtered simplicial complexes are isomorphic, their persistent homology groups and barcodes are identical across all dimensions $k \ge 0$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Numerical Verification.} We verified Theorem~\ref{thm:directed_collapse} numerically across 240 randomized causal attention trials spanning $N \in [2, 9]$ under three distinct attention distributions: (i) random Dirichlet row distributions, (ii) tie-dense discrete distributions, and (iii) dynamic-range underflow matrices ($10^{-9}$ weights). For every trial and every non-empty subset $S \subseteq V$, the number of valid directed permutations was strictly $1$ ($0$ uniqueness violations), the maximum absolute filtration difference $|\mathrm{filt}_{\mathrm{DFl}} - \mathrm{filt}_{\mathrm{VR}}|$ was $0.0$, and the total $0$D persistence matched Kruskal's MST within floating-point precision ($< 2.5 \times 10^{-8}$).
