\newcommand{\RESULTSREDUCTION}{%
Table~\ref{tab:theory} checks Theorem~\ref{thm:cone} on every layer-level graph of every example: \NCellsTotal{} graphs in total. There are \NViolations{} violations of any bound. In the \NSinkSettings{} settings whose models place \SinkMassRange{} of attention on token~0, \PctConedRange{} of graphs are exactly coned ($\defect_0=0$). On each of these graphs, $H_1$ is empty as required by prediction C2. For comparison, $\defect_0$ and $1$D total persistence have Spearman correlation \RhoDeltaHone{} across graphs (C3); the low end is degenerate rather than weak, being the \RhoDeltaHoneNearN{} settings above \RhoDeltaHoneNearCut{} coned, where \RhoDeltaHoneNearHOneZero{} of graphs have no $1$D bars. Elsewhere it is at least \RhoDeltaHoneRest{}. Both $P_0$ and the star weight $\stw_0=(N{-}1)(1-\bar m)$ depend on length, so their raw per-layer rank correlation (median $\ge$\RhoRawMin{}) depends in part on length. Dividing both by $N-1$ gives the median per-layer rank correlation between $P_0/(N-1)$ and $1-\bar m$ at least \RhoMin{} for each sink setting (C1). Length-normalized $0$D total persistence, up to rank, is thus one minus the mean attention paid to the first token in most layers. The single worst individual layer reaches only \RhoLayerMinRange{} per setting. Figure~\ref{fig:layers} locates coning: it happens in the middle of the network, where attention to the sink is high. The first few layers are never sink-dominated, and in some models (TinyLlama, the Qwen models) neither is a block of late layers; these layers are rarely coned. There the length-normalized correlation can fall, as Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}(b) allows once $(N-1)\defect_0$ is comparable to the spread of $\stw_0$ across examples.

\paragraph{Models without a first-token sink as a contrast.} We have \NNoSinkModels{} models with essentially no attention on token~0, \NoSinkModels{}, with \NoSinkMass{} attention there. None of their graphs are coned at $s=0$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:cone} at the first token gives them no useful guarantee. The two are not alike: allowing any apex, \NoSinkConedAnyMin{} of \NoSinkConedAnyMinModel{}'s graphs are coned, so no vertex cones any of them, against \NoSinkConedAnyMax{} of \NoSinkConedAnyMaxModel{}'s, whose attention is strongly concentrated, just not on token~0 (\S\ref{sec:toha}). One model has no cone structure to find; for the other, $s=0$ is the wrong apex. Their $H_1$ is non-empty on \NoSinkHOneNonempty{} of graphs, and the raw $\rho(P_0,\stw_0)$ is high (\RhoNoSinkRaw{}), but nearly all of that comes from shared lengths: the length-normalized correlation is \RhoNoSink{}. Both differ from the sink-dominated models in weights, data and template, so this is a contrast, not a controlled intervention; the within-model intervention is deflation (C4, below).
}

\newcommand{\RESULTSBEYOND}{%
If $0$D persistence reduced exactly to the star, a classifier on the two sink features would match one on the two $0$D features in every layer. Table~\ref{tab:tests} (``Sink vs.\ 0D'') shows the two banks differ by \TOneRange{} AUC across the \NComp{} sink settings evaluated, and are statistically equivalent within $\pm0.015$ in \NTOneEquiv{} of them (the TOST power of this comparison is modest, so non-equivalence is not evidence of a difference). Adding $0$D to the sink bank gains \TTwoRange{}.

\paragraph{The residual lives exactly where the theory says it can.} We split layers without using labels: coned layers ($\ge90\%$ of graphs coned) and open layers ($<50\%$). Across the \SplitN{} sink settings, adding the coned layers' $0$D features to \textsc{Sink} changes AUC by \SplitConed{} (equivalent within $\pm0.015$ in \SplitConedEquiv{} of \SplitConedTotal{}), whereas the open layers account for \SplitOpen{} (Table~\ref{tab:split}). Almost everything $0$D adds beyond sink attention comes from the layers where the sink does not dominate.

\paragraph{Per head, the reduction is tighter.} Per-head sink mass and per-head $0$D are far stronger than their head-averaged versions (Table~\ref{tab:auc}), but adding per-head MST total ($P_{0,h}$) to per-head sink mass changes AUC by only \PHTopoRange{}, which is equivalent within $\pm0.015$ in \NPHTopoEquiv{} of the \NPHTopoTotal{} sink settings with per-head features. The gain is small but not zero: its 95\% interval lies above $0$ in \NPHTopoSig{} of them (at most \PHTopoSigMax{}; Table~\ref{tab:tests}).

\paragraph{The coning defect is a signal, but not a new one.} Theorem~\ref{thm:cone} identifies $\defect_0$ as the unique locus where topology may diverge from the sink, so we evaluate it as a signal (Table~\ref{tab:defect}). Head-averaged, it is weak (AUC \DefLayerAuc{}). Per head, it is strong (\DefHeadAuc{}), with a small improvement when stacked on top of per-head sink mass (\DefHSinkRange{} AUC; 95\% interval above 0 in \DefHSinkSig{} of \DefTotal{} settings). Stacking on top of the non-topological probes yields smaller gains (\DefHNTRange{}; above 0 in \DefHNTSig{}, equivalent within $\pm0.015$ in \DefHNTEquiv{} of \DefTotal{}).

\paragraph{Deleting the sink exposes some, redundant, structure.} Deflation deletes token $0$, so in \NoSinkModels{} it removes a non-sink token rather than the apex; those settings are excluded from the C4 numbers below. Deflated PH (C4) gives a modest gain of \TThreeTQA{} over \textsc{Sink} on TruthfulQA (95\% interval excludes 0 in \NDeflTQASig{} of \NDeflTQATotal{} sink settings) but less on the on-policy benchmark (\TThreeOnp{}; \NDeflOnpSig{} of \NDeflOnpTotal{}). The same holds for answer-only PH (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}). Attention graphs do contain some non-sink geometry, particularly on teacher-forced text. But this geometry is captured by non-topological probes (\S\ref{sec:detection}).
}

\newcommand{\RESULTSONEDIM}{%
Under i.i.d.\ weights, $1$D persistence is thus growing faster than linearly in the lengths relevant for us and linearly asymptotically (Proposition~\ref{prop:morse}; Figure~\ref{fig:synthetic}, left). For real attention, almost no $1$D bars exist (Table~\ref{tab:theory}) and they appear only on graphs with a non-zero coning defect. Figure~\ref{fig:synthetic} (middle) simulates this change in a controlled manner: as we increase the logit bias of the sink, random causal attention goes from looking i.i.d.-like to doing exact coning, while $H_1$ empties out in lockstep.

This explains a common finding in practice, that $1$D features add little to $0$D. At full sample size, adding length-normalized $1$D to $0$D changes AUC by \TFourRange{}, which is equivalent within $\pm0.015$ in \NTFourEquiv{} of \NComp{} sink settings; the exceptions are \TFourExceptions{}; of these, adding $1$D has a significant positive effect on AUC in \TFourHelps{}; the other exception has a negative point estimate but an interval that is too wide to be certain about equality. Note that the improvement seen in TruthfulQA Qwen2.5-3B is also the most sensitive result in terms of inference precision (Appendix~\ref{app:impl}). We want to emphasize that this is not because loops are generally uninformative. For instance, in a strongly sinked model, if a loop exists, there must be some token paying more attention to another token than one of those two tokens is paying to the sink, and the duration of such a loop will be limited by how much extra attention one of them pays to the other. See Figure~\ref{fig:synthetic} (right). $H_1$ can readily find a planted 6-cycle under a moderate sink ($b=6, \gamma=8$: AUC \PlantModerate{}). But a strong sink ($b=8, \gamma\le4$: AUC $\le$\PlantStrong{}) will mask it unless it is strong enough to shatter the cone. Without any sink ($b=0$) $H_1$ will also fail (AUC $\le$\PlantNone{}), since the random attention will already have plenty of background cycles. $1$D persistence is useful only in between.
}

\newcommand{\RESULTSDETECTION}{%
Table~\ref{tab:auc} places attention-graph topology among standard detectors. Three patterns hold across benchmarks.

\emph{Cheap non-topological probes dominate topology.} On TruthfulQA and the on-policy benchmark, the mean-pooled hidden-state probe (\HiddenAll{}) and per-head Lookback ratios (\LookbackAll{}) outperform all topological banks, and combined with log-probability, LLM-Check, and row statistics score \NonTopoAll{}. They are also far wider than the topological banks (Table~\ref{tab:auc}), so this is a claim about what is cheaply available, not about equal capacity; the incremental tests below hold the wide bank fixed. With this combination, adding $0$D PH changes AUC from \IZeroRange{} (equivalent within $\pm0.015$ in \NIZeroEquiv{} of the \NIZeroTotal{} settings with per-head features), and adding sink-deflated PH changes AUC from \IDeflRange{} (equivalent in \NIDeflEquiv{} of \NIZeroTotal{}). To check whether early fusion hides a small bank inside a ${\sim}2{,}000$-dimensional one, we repeat with late fusion. Stacking the $0$D score onto the non-topological score changes AUC by \LFZeroNTRange{} (equivalent in \LFZeroNTEquiv{} of \LFZeroNTTotal{} settings; the 95\% interval lies above 0 in \LFZeroNTSig{} of them), and stacking the deflated-PH score changes it by \LFDeflNTRange{} (equivalent in \LFDeflNTEquiv{} of \LFDeflNTTotal{}). Nested stacking, which refits the first level inside each outer fold and so removes the usual stacking leak, gives \NestZeroNTRange{} for $0$D$\oplus$NT and \NestDeflNTRange{} for deflated PH$\oplus$NT (both equivalent in all \NestZeroNTTotal{} settings with raw features), \NestTohaNTRange{} for TOHA$\oplus$NT, and moves no comparison by more than \NestMaxShift{} (Table~\ref{tab:nested}).

\emph{Which cheap probe wins depends on whether the text is teacher-forced.} Log-probability is weak (\LogpTQA{}) on TruthfulQA, which reflects the benchmark's focus on imitative falsehoods. $0$D PH (\ZeroDTQA{}) outscores \NLogpBeatsZeroDTQA{} of \NLogpZeroDTQATotal{} models, significantly for \NLogpZeroDTQA{}. On-policy, log-probability is strong (\LogpOnp{}) and significantly beats $0$D (\ZeroDOnp{}) for \NLogpOnpSig{} of \NLogpOnpTotal{} models. Single-teacher-forced benchmarks cannot be used to draw conclusions about topology vs.\ confidence.

\emph{Resolution per-head trumps topology.} Head-averaged features are weaker than per-head features of any type, and per-head entropy lies within \PHEntRange{} AUC of per-head $0$D (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}).
}

\newcommand{\RESULTSARTIFACTS}{%
A TF-IDF model on the answer text alone attains \LexTQA{} AUC on TruthfulQA and \LexHE{} on HaluEval, but only \LexOnp{} on-policy: teacher-forced benchmarks reward recognizing how the dataset's answers were written \citep{hussain2026parallax,janiak2025illusion}. We draw no conclusions from HaluEval, where lexical separability is near perfect.
}

\newcommand{\RESULTSDISCUSSION}{%
\paragraph{What attention-graph topology measures.} In decoder-only models, Vietoris--Rips persistence of an attention graph, its stable summaries and TOHA's divergence depend beyond first-order attention on one scalar per graph: the coning defect, zero in most middle layers of every sink model and a bound on the deviation elsewhere (reporting guidelines in Appendix~\ref{app:recs}).
}

\newcommand{\MISTRALAPPENDIX}{%
Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 is the most sink-dominated model in this study, and the reduction is correspondingly tightest there. In its \texttt{[INST]} format it puts \MistralSinkMass{} of its attention on token 0 --- more than any smaller model, and \MistralConed{} of its \MistralCells{} layer graphs are exactly coned ($\defect_0=0$), with \MistralViol{} violations of any bound in Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}.

Its $1$D persistence is exactly zero on \MistralHOneZero{} of its graphs, and $\defect_0=0$ on \MistralConed{}, so near-universal coning is what empties its $1$D bank: adding $1$D to $0$D changes AUC by $0.000$ (Table~\ref{tab:tests}). The length-normalized rank correlation between $P_0/(N{-}1)$ and $1-\bar m$ has median \MistralRhoNorm{} over layers and never drops below \MistralRhoNormMin{}, so on this model $0$D total persistence is, up to rank, an affine function of sink attention in every layer. Per head, \MistralTohaConed{} of its graphs are exactly prompt-coned. Its median $\rho(d,\bar\pi)$ is \MistralTohaRho{}, and Proposition~\ref{prop:toha} has \MistralTohaViol{} violations.
}

\newcommand{\RESULTSTOHA}{%
\emph{Scope.} TOHA was designed for retrieval-augmented generation; only HaluEval supplies a context passage here, so the AUC comparisons below are off-distribution for TOHA and not a reproduction of its reported accuracy. Proposition~\ref{prop:toha} and head-level identity checks hold regardless.

We test Proposition~\ref{prop:toha} on every head of every example: \TohaCells{} head graphs from \TohaSettings{} settings (Table~\ref{tab:toha}). We run TOHA's head-selection algorithm \citep[Alg.~1]{toha} in cross-validation on the whole training fold, for TOHA's score $d$ and the two first-order scores: the $\bar\pi$-score $\tfrac1{|R|}\sum_{u}(1-\pi_u)$ and the response sink score $1-\bar a_0=\tfrac1{|R|}\sum_u(1-A_{u0})$.

\emph{The identity holds on real heads.} The sandwich has \TohaViol{} violations, and our $d$ matches the authors' released \textsc{MTop-Div} code to \NativeMaxDiff{} on \NativeCells{} head graphs (\NativeSettings{} settings; Appendix~\ref{app:validation}). Head graphs are exactly prompt-coned in \TohaConedRange{} of cases (largest $d$--$\bar\pi$ gap \TohaGapMax{}). Median per-head rank correlation $\rho(d,\bar\pi)$ across examples is \TohaRhoRange{}. Against the response sink score it is \TohaRhoSinkRange{} in sink models, but \TohaRhoSinkNoSink{} in \TohaNoSinkModels{} (Table~\ref{tab:tohadiag}), which lack a first-token sink: there $d$ tracks max-prompt attention just as tightly while mildly anti-correlating with token 0. The reduction is to max-prompt attention; that this matches the sink is a property of the model (Appendix~\ref{app:causal}).

\emph{Topology has little effect on the first-order score.} Stacking $d$ onto the $\bar\pi$-score changes AUC by \TohaLFMaxpRange{} ($\pm0.015$ equivalent in \TohaLFMaxpEquiv{}/\TohaLFMaxpTotal{}; 95\% interval $>0$ in \TohaLFMaxpPos{}). Supervised probes on all heads show the $\bar\pi$-score differs from $d$ by \TohaSupMaxpRange{} (equivalent in \TohaSupMaxpEquiv{}/\TohaSupMaxpTotal{}); the response sink score differs by \TohaSupSinkRange{} (equivalent in \TohaSupSinkEquiv{}). Exceptions are models without a first-token sink (\TohaNoSinkModels{}) and one on-policy setting. Discrete head selection is noisier: running on the $\bar\pi$-score shifts AUC by \TohaMaxpRange{} (\TohaMaxpPos{} higher, \TohaMaxpNeg{} lower, \TohaMaxpNonInf{}/\TohaMaxpTotal{} non-inferior at margin 0.015). On the sink score, it loses up to \TohaSinkLossSink{} AUC in sink models (\TohaSinkLossNoSink{} in \TohaNoSinkModels{}): it selects heads prompt-coned on \TohaSelConedRange{} of examples, whereas the sink is the top prompt token on \TohaSelArgZeroRange{} of responses (Table~\ref{tab:tohadiag}). Stacking TOHA onto non-topological features shifts AUC by \TohaLFNTRange{}.

\emph{Why ``attention to $\langle s\rangle$'' looked weak.} \citet[Table~9]{toha} report that attention to the first token is a much weaker feature than their divergence. Proposition~\ref{prop:toha} identifies the quantity that $d$ actually tracks: per head, the attention of the response tokens to their most-attended prompt token. In sink models, that token is the sink for every response token in \TohaArgZeroRange{} of head graphs (Table~\ref{tab:tohadiag}).
}

\newcommand{\CAUSALTEXT}{Proposition~\ref{prop:toha} states that TOHA should correlate more closely with the response sink score the stronger the sink. We can check this directly by intervening on the sink itself. For Qwen2.5-1.5B and TinyLlama on TruthfulQA, we add a constant $b\in\{-2,0,2,4\}$ to the first token's attention logit in every head and recompute all per-head quantities (Table~\ref{tab:causal}). The intervention does not affect the identity (no violations in any run). It affects the structure the proposition says matters. Weakening the sink ($b=-2$) reduces the fraction of prompt-coned head graphs from \CausalQwConedZero{} to \CausalQwConedLo{} (Qwen) and from \CausalTlConedZero{} to \CausalTlConedLo{} (TinyLlama), the fraction where the sink is every response token's top prompt token from \CausalQwArgZero{} to \CausalQwArgLo{} and from \CausalTlArgZero{} to \CausalTlArgLo{}, and the median rank correlation between $d$ and the response sink score from \CausalQwRhoZero{} to \CausalQwRhoLo{} and from \CausalTlRhoZero{} to \CausalTlRhoLo{}. Strengthening it ($b=4$) increases the correlation to \CausalQwRhoHi{} and \CausalTlRhoHi{}. In TinyLlama all three numbers increase monotonically with $b$. In Qwen they peak at $b=2$ and drop a little at $b=4$. TOHA's own AUC hardly changes (\CausalQwTohaRange{} and \CausalTlTohaRange{}). The gap to the sink-score version is not monotone in $b$, showing that our intervention affects TOHA's accuracy through the attention graph rather than being a dose-response.}

\newcommand{\RESULTSRECOMMENDATIONS}{%
(i) Compare a topological detector against the first-order statistic named by the reduction, and report sink attention and $\defect_0$ (or the relevant defect, such as $\defect_P$ for TOHA).
(ii) Compare topological detectors against per-head non-topological statistics (sink mass, entropy, Lookback ratios) at the same resolution, as well as against a hidden-state probe.
(iii) Evaluate on the model's own generations rather than using teacher-forced benchmarks. These mix detection and recognition of dataset authorship.
(iv) If we want to go beyond the sink, either remove the sink first (deflation) or consider only answer tokens. These reveal real non-sink geometry, but in our settings it was not new information. Deflate the token that is actually the apex, which need not be token $0$ (\S\ref{sec:reduction}).
(v) Report the inference precision and the hardware. Short $1$D bars sit at the scale of $\defect_0$, and switching float16 to bfloat16 moves the AUC of $1$D banks by up to \DtypeTopoMax{} while leaving non-topological banks within \DtypeNTMax{} (Appendix~\ref{app:impl}).
}
