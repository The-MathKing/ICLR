\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

Throughout, $D$ is a symmetric $N\times N$ matrix with non-negative entries and zero diagonal, $K_\epsilon$ is the Vietoris--Rips complex at scale $\epsilon$, $\mathrm{filt}_D(\sigma)=\max_{u,v\in\sigma}D_{uv}$ ($\mathrm{filt}_D(\{u\})=0$), and homology is taken over a field. Bars are half-open intervals $[b,d)$.

\subsection{Fact~\ref{fact:mst}}
All vertices enter at $0$. Processing edges in non-decreasing weight order, an edge either joins two components, in which case (elder rule) one $0$D class dies at its weight and Kruskal's algorithm adds the edge to the spanning forest, or it closes a cycle, in which case no $0$D class dies and Kruskal discards it. Hence the $N-1$ finite death times are the MST edge weights \citep{gower1969,edelsbrunner2010}.

\subsection{Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}}

\begin{lemma}[Coned filtrations]
\label{lem:coned}
If $D$ is coned at $s$, then for every $\epsilon\ge0$, $K_\epsilon=C_\epsilon\sqcup I_\epsilon$, where $C_\epsilon$ is a simplicial cone with apex $s$ and $I_\epsilon$ is a set of isolated vertices. Consequently $\tilde H_k(K_\epsilon)=0$ for every $k\ge1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\sigma\in K_\epsilon$ with $s\notin\sigma$ and $|\sigma|\ge2$. For each $u\in\sigma$ choose $v\in\sigma\setminus\{u\}$; coning gives $D_{su}\le D_{uv}\le\mathrm{filt}_D(\sigma)\le\epsilon$. Therefore $\mathrm{filt}_D(\sigma\cup\{s\})=\mathrm{filt}_D(\sigma)$ and $\sigma\cup\{s\}\in K_\epsilon$. Now let $u\ne s$ be a vertex with $D_{su}>\epsilon$. If $u$ had an edge $\{u,v\}\in K_\epsilon$ then $D_{su}\le D_{uv}\le\epsilon$, a contradiction; so $u$ is isolated. Let $I_\epsilon$ be these vertices and $C_\epsilon$ the subcomplex on the remaining vertices. Every vertex of $C_\epsilon$ is adjacent to $s$, and by the first part every simplex of $C_\epsilon$ extends by $s$, so $C_\epsilon$ is the cone over its link of $s$ and is contractible. $H_k$ of a disjoint union is the direct sum, and isolated points have no homology in degree $\ge1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}]
Fix $s$ and $\defect=\defect_s(D)$. Define $D'_{uv}=\max(D_{uv},D_{su},D_{sv})$ for $u,v\ne s$, $u\ne v$; $D'_{su}=D'_{us}=D_{su}$; $D'_{uu}=0$.

\emph{Step 1: $D\le D'\le D+\defect$ and $D'$ is coned at $s$.} The first inequality is immediate. For $u,v\ne s$, $D'_{uv}-D_{uv}=[\max(D_{su},D_{sv})-D_{uv}]_+\le\defect$. Coning: $\max(D'_{su},D'_{sv})=\max(D_{su},D_{sv})\le D'_{uv}$.

\emph{Step 2: interleaving.} Since filtration values are maxima of entries, $\mathrm{filt}_D\le\mathrm{filt}_{D'}\le\mathrm{filt}_D+\defect$. Hence for every $b$, $K_b\subseteq K'_{b+\defect}\subseteq K_{b+\defect}$, where $K'$ is the filtration of $D'$.

\emph{Step 3: (a).} The persistence map $H_k(K_b)\to H_k(K_{b+\defect})$ is induced by the composite inclusion in Step 2 and therefore factors through $H_k(K'_{b+\defect})$, which is zero for $k\ge1$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:coned}. The rank of $H_k(K_b)\to H_k(K_{b'})$ equals the number of bars $[b_i,d_i)$ with $b_i\le b$ and $d_i>b'$. A bar with $d_i-b_i>\defect$ would contribute to this rank at $b=b_i$, $b'=b_i+\defect$, a contradiction. So every bar has length at most $\defect$, and summing gives the bound on $P_k$.

\emph{Step 4: (b).} By Fact~\ref{fact:mst}, $P_0(D)=w_D(T_D)$ for an MST $T_D$ of $D$. The star $T_s=\{\{s,u\}\}_{u\ne s}$ is a spanning tree, so $P_0(D)\le w_D(T_s)=\stw_s(D)$. For the lower bound, $T_s$ is an MST of $D'$, even with ties. Root any spanning tree $T$ at $s$ and let $e_u$ be the edge from $u\ne s$ to its parent $p(u)$. If $p(u)=s$ then $D'_{e_u}=D'_{su}$; otherwise $D'_{e_u}=D'_{u\,p(u)}\ge\max(D'_{su},D'_{s\,p(u)})\ge D'_{su}$ because $D'$ is coned. The map $u\mapsto e_u$ is a bijection onto the edges of $T$, so $w_{D'}(T)\ge\sum_{u\ne s}D'_{su}=w_{D'}(T_s)$ and $\max_{e\in T}D'_e\ge\max_u D'_{su}$. Hence $w_{D'}(T_{D'})=w_{D'}(T_s)=\stw_s(D)$. The tree $T_D$ spans $s$, so at least one of its $N-1$ edges is incident to $s$, where $D'=D$; each of the other at most $N-2$ edges satisfies $D'_e\le D_e+\defect$. Therefore
$P_0(D)=w_D(T_D)\ge w_{D'}(T_D)-(N-2)\defect\ge w_{D'}(T_{D'})-(N-2)\defect=\stw_s(D)-(N-2)\defect.$
The maximum finite $0$D death is the bottleneck value $\min_T\max_{e\in T}w(e)$, which is attained by any MST; it is at most $\max_u D_{su}$ (the star is a spanning tree) and is $1$-Lipschitz in the sup-norm, which gives the lower bound because the bottleneck of $D'$ is $\max_u D_{su}$ (by the rooted-tree argument above).

\emph{Relation to known results.} Lemma~\ref{lem:coned} is the classical observation that a flag complex in which one vertex is adjacent to every non-isolated vertex, and to every simplex, is a cone; this is the dominated-vertex mechanism behind strong collapses \citep{barmak2012strong} and edge collapses of flag filtrations \citep{boissonnat2020edge}. Step~3 is a one-sided instance of the interleaving argument behind the stability of persistence \citep{cohensteiner2007stability,chazal2009proximity,chazal2014geometric}. The two-sided stability theorem would give bars of length at most $2\defect$, whereas the one-sided comparison $D\le D'$ gives $\defect$, which is attained (Section~\ref{sec:theory}).

\emph{Step 5: (c).} If $\defect=0$ then $D'=D$, and the claims follow from Lemma~\ref{lem:coned} and Step 4.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:diagram}]
Let $D'$ be as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}. Since $D'$ is coned at $s$, Lemma~\ref{lem:coned} and Step~4 give $\dgm_k(D')=\Sigma_s(D)_k$ for all $k$: no bars in degree $k\ge1$, and $0$D deaths equal to the star weights $D'_{su}=D_{su}$. For $1\le j\le N-1$, the $j$-th smallest finite $0$D death of a matrix $E$ is $w_j(E)=\min\{\epsilon:\beta_0(K^E_\epsilon)\le N-j\}$. If $E\le E'$ entrywise then $K^{E'}_\epsilon\subseteq K^E_\epsilon$ on the same vertex set, so $\beta_0(K^E_\epsilon)\le\beta_0(K^{E'}_\epsilon)$ and $w_j(E)\le w_j(E')$; and $w_j(E+c)=w_j(E)+c$ on off-diagonal entries. From $D\le D'\le D+\defect_s$ we get $w_j(D)\le D_{s(j)}\le w_j(D)+\defect_s$. The same inclusions give the Betti-$0$ sandwich, because $\beta_0(K'_\epsilon)=1+\#\{u:D_{su}>\epsilon\}$. Matching the $j$-th deaths shows that $d_B(\dgm_0(D),\Sigma_s(D)_0)\le\defect_s$. Matching them instead to $t_j=(D_{s(j)}-\defect_s/2)_+$ costs at most $\defect_s/2$ each: if $D_{s(j)}\ge\defect_s/2$ then $w_j\in[D_{s(j)}-\defect_s,D_{s(j)}]=[t_j-\defect_s/2,t_j+\defect_s/2]$, and otherwise $t_j=0\le w_j\le D_{s(j)}<\defect_s/2$. For $W_1$, all differences $D_{s(j)}-w_j$ are non-negative, so the cost of the sorted matching is $\sum_j(D_{s(j)}-w_j)=\stw_s(D)-P_0(D)\le(N-2)\defect_s$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}(b). In degree $k\ge1$, every bar has length at most $\defect_s$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}(a)), so matching it to the diagonal costs at most $\defect_s/2$ in $\ell^\infty$; this gives $d_B(\dgm_k(D),\emptyset)\le\defect_s/2$ and the $W_1$ term $\tfrac12\defect_s|\dgm_k(D)|$. The Lipschitz statements follow from the stability of landscapes in $d_B$ \citep{bubenik2015landscapes} and of persistence images in $W_1$ \citep{adams2017persistence}. If $\defect_s=0$ then $D=D'$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:toha}]
Let $\tilde D$ equal $D$ except that $\tilde D_{vv'}=0$ for $v,v'\in P$. At scale $0$, Kruskal's algorithm joins all of $P$ into one component with $|P|-1$ zero-length bars. Every later merge is a merge in the quotient graph $Q$ on $\{p^*\}\cup R$ with $Q_{p^*u}=\min_{v\in P}\tilde D_{vu}$ and $Q_{uw}=D_{uw}$. So $\mathrm{MTopDiv}(R,P)=P_0(Q)$. For $u\in R$ and $v\in P$ we have $v<u$ and $D_{uv}=1-A_{uv}$, so $Q_{p^*u}=1-\pi_u$. For $w<u$ in $R$, $Q_{uw}=1-A_{uw}$, and $\max(Q_{p^*u},Q_{p^*w})-Q_{uw}=A_{uw}-\min(\pi_u,\pi_w)$, so $\defect_{p^*}(Q)=\defect_P$. $Q$ has $|R|+1$ vertices, so Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}(b) gives $\sum_u(1-\pi_u)-(|R|-1)\defect_P\le P_0(Q)\le\sum_u(1-\pi_u)$, with equality when $\defect_P=0$ by part (c). Divide by $|R|$. Finally $\pi_u\ge A_{u0}$ because $0\in P$.
\end{proof}

We checked both statements numerically. The fast MTop-Div (a dense Prim's algorithm on $Q$) agrees with ripser run on $\tilde D$ to $\CheckTohaErr$ on \CheckTohaN{} random causal matrices. On these, and on \CheckDiagN{} further random matrices checked for the elementwise death bound, the $(N{-}2)\defect$ sandwich, the $\defect/2$ shifted-star matching, and the bar bound in degrees $1$ and $2$, there are \CheckViol{} violations (\texttt{sinktda/check\_theory.py}). The tightened lower bounds also hold on real attention, with \TightViol{} violations on \TightLayerGraphs{} layer graphs (\TightLayerSettings{} settings) and \TightHeadGraphs{} head graphs (\TightHeadSettings{} settings) whose per-example features we retain (\texttt{sinktda/check\_tight.py}); Table~\ref{tab:boundgap} shows how much of the slack real graphs use.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:causal}]
For $u>v$, $A_{vu}=0$, so $D_{uv}=1-A_{uv}$; in particular $D_{0u}=1-A_{u0}$. For $1\le v<u$, $\max(D_{0u},D_{0v})-D_{uv}=A_{uv}-\min(A_{u0},A_{v0})$. Substitute into Theorem~\ref{thm:cone}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Linear growth of $1$D persistence under i.i.d.\ weights}
The usual reason for length normalizing $1$D features is that there is growth in cycles with $N$. This is understood if weights are i.i.d.: $0$D total persistence tends to $\zeta(3)$ \citep{frieze1985mst} and $1$D total persistence of the random clique-complex process is $\Theta(N)$ \citep{hiraoka2017msa,hino2019lifetime}. Here is a simple bound that makes explicit the constant.

\begin{proposition}[Linear growth under i.i.d.\ weights]
\label{prop:morse}
If $\{D_{uv}\}_{u<v}$ are i.i.d.\ $\mathrm{Uniform}[0,1]$, then
$\mathbb{E}[P_1(N)]\ \ge\ \int_0^1\big[\tbinom N2p-N-\tbinom N3p^3\big]_+dp\ =\ \tfrac{3N}{8}-\sqrt{3N}+O(1)$. (There is also an $O(N)$ upper bound in Theorem~1.5 of \citealp{hino2019lifetime}.)
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With i.i.d.\ uniform weights, the edges with $D_{uv}\le p$ form an Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi graph $G(N,p)$ and $K_p$ is its clique (flag) complex. Every $1$D bar is contained in $[0,1)$, because $K_1$ is the full simplex. Writing $\beta_1(p)$ for the first Betti number of $K_p$, the number of bars alive at $p$ is $\beta_1(p)$, so $P_1=\int_0^1\beta_1(p)\,dp$.
For any finite complex with $f_i$ faces of dimension $i$, the strong Morse inequality at degree $2$ reads $\beta_2-\beta_1+\beta_0\le f_2-f_1+f_0$ \citep{edelsbrunner2010}, hence $\beta_1\ge f_1-f_0-f_2+\beta_0+\beta_2\ge f_1-f_0-f_2$. Since also $\beta_1\ge0$, Jensen's inequality gives $\mathbb{E}\beta_1(p)\ge[\mathbb{E}f_1-f_0-\mathbb{E}f_2]_+=[\binom N2p-N-\binom N3p^3]_+$. Integrating over $p$ gives the first inequality. With $p=x/\sqrt N$ and $dp=dx/\sqrt N$, the integrand divided by $\sqrt N$ is $N(x/2-x^3/6)-\sqrt N+O(1)$ uniformly for $x\in[0,\sqrt3+1]$. It is positive on an interval whose endpoints are $O(N^{-1/2})$ from $0$ and $\sqrt3$, and it is negative for larger $x$. Hence
$\int_0^1[\cdot]_+dp=N\int_0^{\sqrt3}\big(\tfrac x2-\tfrac{x^3}{6}\big)dx-\sqrt{3N}+O(1)=\tfrac{3N}{8}-\sqrt{3N}+O(1).$
Numerically, $(\tfrac{3N}{8}-\text{bound})/\sqrt N=1.62,1.67,1.70,1.72$ at $N=2^8,2^{10},2^{12},2^{14}$, approaching $\sqrt3\approx1.73$. The order $\Theta(N)$ itself is known \citep{hiraoka2017msa,hino2019lifetime}; the proposition only makes the constant explicit.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Directed flag complexes on causal attention}
\label{app:directed}
\begin{proposition}[Directed flag collapse]
\label{thm:directed}
Let $A$ be lower-triangular with non-negative entries (self-loops are ignored). Let $\mathrm{DFl}(A)$ be the filtered directed flag complex \citep{flagser2020} of the digraph with an edge $i\to j$ for \emph{every} $j<i$ (a transitive tournament) and weight $1-A_{ij}$, and let $\VR(D)$ be as above with $D=1-\max(A,A^\top)$. These filtered complexes are isomorphic, so $\dgm_k(\mathrm{DFl}(A))=\dgm_k(\VR(D))$ for all $k$. This remains true for any weighting $w(i\to j)=\phi(A_{ij})$ with $D_{ij}=\phi(\max(A_{ij},A_{ji}))$ and $\phi$ non-increasing.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A directed $k$-simplex is an ordered tuple $(v_0,\dots,v_k)$ with an edge $v_a\to v_b$ for all $a<b$. Edges exist only from later to earlier tokens, so a vertex set $S$ supports exactly one directed simplex, its elements in decreasing order; every other ordering uses a missing forward edge. (A missing edge is not a zero-weight edge; encoding it as weight $0$ would admit all orderings, a common implementation error.) This bijection preserves faces. Its filtration value is $\max_{a<b}(1-A_{v_av_b})$, and for $u>v$, $D_{uv}=1-A_{uv}$, so the undirected value $\max_{u,v\in S}D_{uv}$ is the same number.
\end{proof}
We confirm the bijection and the filtration identity explicitly on $420$ random causal matrices ($N\le8$, four families including tie-dense and underflowing matrices; $30{,}060$ vertex sets; zero violations) and check that it does not hold for bidirectional attention, meaning that a set of size $n$ has $n!$ orderings. The collapse comes from causality: for decoders, adding the directed flag complex brings nothing to the symmetrized one. This statement does not cover directional features that are not coming from flag complexes, like \citet{kushnareva2021} for bidirectional encoders.

\section{Implementation and protocol details}
\label{app:impl}

\paragraph{Extraction.} Every setting runs in bfloat16 with eager attention and no quantization. The ones using the two 7B models (Mistral-7B on both benchmarks, its chat-format variant, and Qwen2.5-7B on both benchmarks) have been extracted on an NVIDIA RTX 5080 (CUDA), while others were extracted on Apple-silicon (MPS), where float16 produces non-finite values and degenerate generations for Qwen models and the extractor refuses non-finite logits. The paragraph on float32 below measures how much the platform itself moves a reported AUC. Model identifiers are in Table~\ref{tab:models} (Hugging Face main revisions as of September 2026). For each example we run one forward pass with attentions and hidden states, average heads, and form $D$ in float64. Ripser \citep{bauer2021ripser} computes $0$D/$1$D barcodes; since SciPy's sparse MST can omit near-zero-weight edges ($A_{u0}\approx1$), we compute $0$D MST summaries using a batched dense Prim's algorithm. The $0$D bank is (max lifetime, total persistence) per layer; the $1$D bank is (max lifetime, total persistence, birth of the longest bar, death of the longest bar) per layer. Only the $1$D summaries are divided by a length: $1$D and \textsc{Deflated}'s $1$D block by the sequence length $N$, \textsc{Answer-only}'s $1$D block by $\max(\text{answer tokens},1)$. Everything else, including the $0$D, \textsc{Deflated} $0$D and \textsc{Sink} blocks, enters the classifier raw. That is deliberate: Theorem~\ref{thm:cone} pairs raw $P_0$ with raw $\stw_0$, and normalizing one of the two would compare banks that differ in their length content rather than in their topology. Both therefore carry the sequence length, which correlates with the label in some settings (Table~\ref{tab:lengths}); \textsc{Len} (sequence and answer length alone) is scored as a separate bank in every setting for exactly that reason, and it is far below the topological banks everywhere except HaluEval (Table~\ref{tab:auc_full}). \textsc{Legacy mstproxy}, which appears in Tables~\ref{tab:auc_full} and~\ref{tab:tests_full}, is the per-layer $0$D total persistence on its own (one feature per layer): the MST-weight summary used by earlier attention-graph work, without the max-lifetime feature. $\defect_0$ applies the closed form of Corollary~\ref{cor:causal}; we brute force compute $\min_s\defect_s$ over all apexes. \textsc{Deflated} deletes token 0 and renormalizes each row over the remaining causal support. This is a first-token deletion, not an apex deletion: in the \NNoSinkModels{} models without a first-token sink (\NoSinkModels{}) the apex is elsewhere, so their \textsc{Deflated} features remove an ordinary token and are excluded from the C4 numbers in \S\ref{sec:beyond}. Every setting's per-layer apex is recorded (\texttt{delta\_argmin}); Appendix\ref{app:apex} re-extracts \ApexSettings{} settings deflating that apex instead, which is the experiment C4 actually calls for. \textsc{Answer-only} limits $D$ to the answer tokens and does not renormalize. The Lookback ratio of head $h$ is the mean over answer tokens $t$ of $\bar a_{\mathrm{ctx}}/(\bar a_{\mathrm{ctx}}+\bar a_{\mathrm{new}})$, where $\bar a$ is the mean attention from $t$ to prompt tokens and to answer tokens up to $t$. The LLM-Check score of a head is the mean log diagonal attention (the log-determinant of the triangular attention kernel divided by $N$), summed over heads per layer. Log-probability features are the mean, minimum, and sum of answer-token log-probabilities, mean and maximum predictive entropy, and mean maximum softmax probability. Hidden states are mean-pooled over answer tokens at layer $\lfloor L/2\rfloor$.

\paragraph{Prompts.} For TruthfulQA we use each model's own format: the Qwen chat template with an explicit system turn ``You are a helpful assistant.'', \texttt{<|user|>\ldots<|end|>} for Phi-3, \texttt{<|user|>\ldots</s>} for TinyLlama, ChatML without a system turn for SmolLM, and \texttt{[INST]\ldots[/INST]} for Mistral. HaluEval uses \texttt{Knowledge: \ldots\textbackslash nQuestion: \ldots\textbackslash nAnswer: } without a chat template. The boundary is computed by tokenizing the prefix up to but not including the final space, so that the space is attached to the first answer token when the full string is tokenized. When the chat template itself outputs the BOS token (e.g., Mistral's own template, Llama-3.1), it does not output a second one. Both the prefix and the full string are then tokenized in the same way, meaning the token ids of the prefix are a prefix of the full string's. This is verified for all models and templates without a GPU in \texttt{sinktda/check\_tokenization.py}. HaluEval's raw format is the format that benchmark ships with, and it is what makes the prompt directly comparable across models, but the models we run on it are instruction-tuned, so it puts them outside the format they were tuned on and may change their sink behaviour; we keep it for comparability and draw no conclusion from HaluEval that we do not also draw from a chat-formatted benchmark (\S\ref{sec:detection}). The on-policy prompt is the model's chat template around ``Answer the following question with a short phrase only, no explanation. Question: \ldots''. The token at position 0 is the first token of the format (BOS for TinyLlama and Mistral, \texttt{<|user|>} for Phi-3, whose tokenizer adds no BOS, \texttt{<|im\_start|>} for Qwen and SmolLM, and ``Knowledge'' for Qwen on HaluEval). The theorem holds for any value, not necessarily a special token. We generate on-policy answers using the chat-formatted prompt with \texttt{add\_special\_tokens=False} and extract features with the tokenizer's default special tokens. They only differ for TinyLlama, where the input to feature extraction begins with a BOS token. Mistral runs with its hand-written \texttt{[INST]} string and with its own chat template. The two prefixes tokenize identically; the settings differ only in the template's end-of-sequence token. Banks that do not rely on the answer window agree to \TwinMaxOther{} AUC. The answer-restricted banks disagree more (answer-only PH by \TwinAns{}, deflated PH by \TwinDefl{}), since that token becomes part of the answer subgraph and the deflated graph.

\paragraph{Statistics.} Bootstrap resamples are drawn over questions (both rows of a pair move together). TOST power at margin $m$ is under a true difference of 0 and a normal sampling distribution with the bootstrap SE: $2\Phi(m/\mathrm{SE}-z_{0.95})-1$. Average repeated-CV predictions (seeds 42, 43, 44) are used for bootstrapping (seed 0), i.e., the intervals report sampling of questions with a fixed, split-averaged predictor without refitting variance. Seed-to-seed SD of the pooled AUC is given separately (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}). It is at most \MaxSeedSD{}, with \NSeedSDAbove{} out of the bank--setting cells exceeding $0.004$. The $C$ rule is a function of bank width alone: banks of at most 64 dimensions are fit at the fixed $C=1$, wider banks at a $C$ chosen from $\{0.01,0.1,1\}$ by inner grouped 3-fold CV on the training fold. Because the rule depends only on width, the same bank always receives the same regularization treatment in every comparison it enters. That grid is narrow for the widest banks, and an under-regularized baseline would understate exactly the comparisons the paper leans on, so we repeated every reported bank in each of the \CGridN{} settings whose features we retain with the wider grid $\{10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1},1,10,10^{2}\}$, applied to every bank regardless of width (Table~\ref{tab:cgrid}). The non-topological banks, which are the ones the narrow grid could have disadvantaged, gain at most \CGridNTMax{} (hidden-state probe \CGridHiddenMax{}, full non-topological bank \CGridNTBankMax{}), so they were not materially under-regularized. The topological banks gain more, up to \CGridTopoMax{} (\CGridWorst{}); the wider grid therefore favours the topological side of every comparison, and the conclusions below are the ones that survive that. Answer-only PH gains the most and still overtakes the non-topological bank in \CGridAnsOverNT{} of the settings where that bank is available, and across all settings the four dominance orderings the paper claims (\textsc{Nontopo}${}\ge{}$\textsc{0D}, \textsc{Nontopo}${}\ge{}$\textsc{Defl}, \textsc{Hidden}${}\ge{}$\textsc{0D}, \textsc{Nontopo}${}\ge{}$\textsc{PH-0D}) reverse \CGridFlips{} times. The reduction's own comparison is a difference of two banks rather than either one, and $\mathrm{AUC}(0\text{D})-\mathrm{AUC}(\textsc{Sink})$ moves by at most \CGridTOneShiftMax{}, in the direction of the sink. The conclusions are not an artefact of the grid. Late fusion trains the second-level model on first-level out-of-fold scores as in regular stacking. Training rows' first-level scores are obtained by training models which have seen the corresponding test questions, which is a weak, standard optimism that both arms of the comparison share. Nested stacking removes it: within each outer training fold, every first-level model is refit on inner grouped 5-fold splits of that fold only, the second-level model is trained on those inner out-of-fold scores, and it is applied to the test fold's scores from a first-level model fit on the whole training fold. No input to the stacker then depends on a test label. Table~\ref{tab:nested} reports it on the \NestN{} settings whose raw features we retain (\NestList{}); it moves no stacked comparison by more than \NestMaxShift{} AUC.

\section{Deflating the apex instead of token 0}
\label{app:apex}

Prediction C4 says that removing the sink should expose whatever non-sink topology exists, and \textsc{Deflated} tests it by deleting token 0. That is the right vertex only when token 0 is the apex. Across the \ApexSettings{} settings re-extracted here (\ApexList{}), token 0 is the per-layer apex $\arg\min_s\defect_s$ in \ApexIsZeroRange{} of (example, layer) graphs: \ApexIsZeroMax{} in the sink-dominated control and \ApexIsZeroMin{} in the model without a first-token sink, where deflation had therefore been deleting an ordinary token every time. We re-extract with \texttt{-{}-apex-deflation}, which deletes each layer's own apex, and evaluate into a separate results directory so the reported tables are untouched.

\paragraph{The correction matters, and it matters where the theory says.} Against \textsc{Sink}, apex deflation adds \ApexBeyondSinkRange{} AUC where token-0 deflation adds \DeflBeyondSinkRange{}. The two agree in the control, where they are usually the same deletion (\ApexVsDeflEquiv{} of \ApexVsDeflTotal{} settings equivalent within $\pm0.015$, overall range \ApexVsDeflRange{}), and differ most in the model without a first-token sink, where deflating the apex is worth \ApexVsDeflMax{} AUC over deflating token 0 (\ApexVsDeflMaxSetting{}, 95\% interval above zero). A first-token deletion therefore understates non-sink structure in exactly the models whose apex is not token 0, which is where C4 needed testing.

\paragraph{It does not change the conclusion.} Stacked onto the non-topological probes, correctly apex-deflated PH changes AUC by \ApexBeyondNTRange{}, equivalent within $\pm0.015$ in \ApexBeyondNTEquiv{} of \ApexBeyondNTTotal{} settings. Fixing the deflation makes it a better topological feature and leaves it just as redundant against cheap first-order probes. The apex is chosen per layer from the same graph the features are computed on, so this is a label-free but data-dependent choice of vertex: read it as an intervention that tests C4, not as a detector competing on AUC.
